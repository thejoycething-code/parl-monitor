# MPs Reject Assisted Dying Bill at Second Reading

The House of Commons voted to reject the Terminally Ill Adults (End of Life) Bill at Second Reading on 11 September 2026, negativing the motion "That the Bill now be read a Second time" by 286 votes to 270. Earlier in the sitting MPs had agreed, by 293 votes to 218, to a closure motion under Standing Order No. 36 allowing the Second Reading question to be put.

Of the 75 members who spoke, 46 went on to vote against the Bill, from Labour, the Conservatives, the Liberal Democrats, the Democratic Unionist Party, Alliance and the independent benches. Those quoted below spoke against a Bill which would allow doctors to prescribe lethal drugs to terminally ill adults assessed as having six months or less to live. They warned of insufficient safeguards against coercion, the state of palliative and social care, and the impact on disabled and vulnerable people. Other members, from Labour, the Conservatives, the Liberal Democrats and the Greens, spoke in favour, arguing the Bill extended choice and dignity at the end of life and had already been heavily scrutinised.

Responding for the Government, [Alison McGovern](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-2D09DD64-AC1B-4FF5-BD5F-17E6FC398D49), the Minister for Social Care, said: "The Government remain neutral on whether the Bill should become law, but it is our serious responsibility, should Parliament pass it, to ensure effective implementation within the framework provided by the legislation."

The most forceful contribution against the Bill came from [Ashley Dalton](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-B2BF97D5-6BF1-4BC4-AA24-E8ACB09E4579), Labour MP for West Lancashire, who disclosed she is living with stage 4 metastatic breast cancer. She said: "I do not know how long I will live. I will be on treatment for life, however long or short that may be. At the moment, I live between scans, in nine to 12-week blocks of time... The six-month prognosis in this Bill is something no one can ever be really sure of." She concluded starkly: "A terrible death or an assisted death is not a choice; it is a threat."

[Carla Lockhart](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-8F53EAD4-5B07-4FA1-B636-B7484BB5C54E), DUP MP for Upper Bann, told the House: "The soft language of 'assisted dying' is misleading the public, because it obscures the stark and very dark reality of what is actually being proposed... it is a Bill that allows doctors to provide lethal drugs for a patient to end their life. Let the reality of that sink in: our great NHS, the principles of which are to protect and to save lives, would be responsible for state-sponsored suicide. That is what it is."

[Dr Andrew Murrison](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-8CAF63DE-0066-4097-B988-23B17ECA405E), Conservative MP for South West Wiltshire, said: "The first reason why I will not support the Bill today is that I seriously do not want to live in the kind of society in which the frail, elderly and vulnerable feel coerced into 'doing the right thing.' We can set up panels, and involve judges in chambers, social workers and all the rest of it, but as humans, we know how humans work. We know the light side and the dark."

[Shivani Raja](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-AE24A6A6-58E4-4FF1-B284-7D02C982EA83), Conservative MP for Leicester East, raised cultural pressures on decision-making, warning "I will not support any Bill that leaves them vulnerable to coercion." [Dame Karen Bradley](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-A638C000-65BD-4898-9934-A02379900686), Conservative MP for Staffordshire Moorlands, asked colleagues: "Are you prepared today to say that this Bill, in this form, is safe to be forced into law?" [Dr Zubir Ahmed](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-0EE0E183-1ED3-4E26-84E7-C1F3A62FABFA), Labour MP for Glasgow South West, said the Bill "is less about relieving suffering and more about bypassing its possibility." [Josh Fenton-Glynn](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-3CDABD9C-3139-4B13-B507-FD67BDD1A886), Labour MP for Calder Valley, concluded "with a heavy heart, not this Bill and not at this time." [Sarah Smith](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-7C69FC6E-3720-48AB-AAD7-96F49894C61F), Labour MP for Hyndburn, argued "Assisted dying is not a choice if palliative care is not provided as an alternative." [Mims Davies](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-1D335F62-83F8-4A8D-96A2-ECFACAE98DA6), Conservative MP for East Grinstead and Uckfield, drawing on her father's disablement, said "this process is completely wrong."

Speaking in favour of the Bill, [Lauren Edwards](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-6600EC35-4896-4BB4-BEC0-DE62BD9B7E49), Labour MP for Rochester and Strood, said "the will of this Chamber should be respected." [Alicia Kearns](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-1AB111F8-233E-4EC7-8966-C6503D5ED794), Conservative MP for Rutland and Stamford, said "There is no harm in the principle of assisted dying." [Kit Malthouse](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-DD6C4B4E-390D-4A46-ABB5-FAD9B61B249F), Conservative MP for North West Hampshire, said MPs could "give them mercy and love." [Pippa Heylings](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-F3EB5383-79F7-4ECD-ACAF-592E1035AF9E), Liberal Democrat MP for South Cambridgeshire, said "This Bill will not make a choice for anyone." [Tom Gordon](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-92AA7718-9E10-4833-9800-7471AF83EBE7), Liberal Democrat MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, said MPs were "restarting a process that was abruptly ended by those in the other place." [Liz Kendall](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-D0E04AEF-A214-47BE-A471-6A2723925F86), Labour MP for Leicester West, called it "the most scrutinised Bill I have seen in my 16 years in Parliament."
