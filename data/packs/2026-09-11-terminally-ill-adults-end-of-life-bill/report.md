# Commons Rejects Assisted Dying Bill At Second Reading, 270 To 286

The House of Commons rejected the Terminally Ill Adults (End of Life) Bill on 11 September 2026, voting 270 to 286 against giving the Bill a Second Reading. Earlier in the sitting, MPs had voted 293 to 218 to agree a closure motion bringing the debate to a close, before going on to defeat the Bill itself later the same afternoon.

Eleven MPs spoke against the Bill, from Labour, the Conservatives and the Liberal Democrats, while five spoke in favour of it. As described by MPs in the debate, the Bill would have allowed a person with a terminal diagnosis and six months or less to live to be prescribed lethal drugs for self-administration, following assessment by two doctors and a panel and a 14-day period intended to allow reflection.

Several members who opposed the Bill, including those who had supported earlier versions of it, argued that the text before the House was unchanged in substance from the Bill previously passed by the Commons and sent to the Lords, and that no guarantee existed that further amendments would be accepted if it proceeded. No Minister's response to the debate was recorded among the speeches supplied.

The strongest intervention against the Bill came from [Ashley Dalton](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-B2BF97D5-6BF1-4BC4-AA24-E8ACB09E4579), Labour MP for West Lancashire, who disclosed that she is living with stage 4 metastatic breast cancer:

> "I would be lying if I said that when thinking about all that was to come, I did not consider that it might be fairer and easier on everyone if I just got the dying over with as soon as possible. Having treatable depression, however, will not exclude anyone from an assisted death under this Bill... People have been terrorised—I have been terrorised—with tales of people vomiting up their own faeces, as though this is commonplace during death. It is vanishingly rare... A terrible death or an assisted death is not a choice; it is a threat."

[Monica Harding](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-0F91E9A5-9684-4138-9A8C-CEAEE4F11A37), Liberal Democrat MP for Esher and Walton, described losing two siblings with poor mental health during the Bill's passage:

> "This Bill is not contingent on making palliative care provision or mental health provision better, and there is no provision in this Bill to assess unmet psychological medical needs at any stage... The offer of assisted dying to people in the position my siblings were in is the very opposite of autonomy... Today, we must vote no."

[Dame Karen Bradley](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-A638C000-65BD-4898-9934-A02379900686), Conservative MP for Staffordshire Moorlands, told colleagues who had previously backed the Bill that changing their vote required no repudiation of principle:

> "You do not have to decide that assisted dying can never be right... You need only ask whether this Bill, in this form, on the evidence before us today, is safe enough for Parliament to force it into law. That is today's test."

[Dr Zubir Ahmed](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-0EE0E183-1ED3-4E26-84E7-C1F3A62FABFA), Labour MP for Glasgow South West, drawing on his experience as a doctor, said "the trouble is that neither has to know the patient well, or go to any great lengths to exclude the likelihood of coercion. Doctors are trained to do many things, but assessing and picking up on coercion is not one of them." [Sir Bernard Jenkin](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-47B5A629-42F8-477C-87E0-BFCA61BFDE95), Conservative MP for Harwich and North Essex, said the Bill "keeps changing" and urged colleagues to "vote against the Bill today." [Shivani Raja](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-AE24A6A6-58E4-4FF1-B284-7D02C982EA83), Conservative MP for Leicester East, warned that in some cultures "decision-making is a collective process involving family and community members," raising concerns about coercion in her constituency's diverse communities.

[Sarah Smith](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-7C69FC6E-3720-48AB-AAD7-96F49894C61F), Labour MP for Hyndburn, said the Bill would "almost certainly further increase the gap in life expectancy between the richest and poorest." [Julie Minns](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-54CE56D9-A367-4C30-B84D-25758E3D2341), Labour MP for Carlisle, said "we have a duty as legislators to ensure that the resource, funding and support is there for people" before facilitating death. [Janet Daby](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-0849DAC1-A236-434B-B849-C7CC04B619C9), Labour MP for Lewisham East, who reversed her earlier support, said "I decided that if the opportunity arose again, I would vote against assisted dying." [Mims Davies](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-1D335F62-83F8-4A8D-96A2-ECFACAE98DA6), Conservative MP for East Grinstead and Uckfield, said "this process is completely wrong," citing her disabled father's fear that "he was worth more to us dead than alive." [Damian Hinds](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-7B0C8A66-C48C-4252-AE35-B401740B28F0), Conservative MP for East Hampshire, said MPs must weigh "the evidence and, crucially, the legislation before us as we cast our votes."

Five MPs argued the other way. [Alicia Kearns](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-1AB111F8-233E-4EC7-8966-C6503D5ED794), Conservative MP for Rutland and Stamford, said "there is no harm in the principle of assisted dying, and I am yet to hear an argument that exposes it." [Pippa Heylings](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-F3EB5383-79F7-4ECD-ACAF-592E1035AF9E), Liberal Democrat MP for South Cambridgeshire, said "this Bill will not make a choice for anyone. It will simply allow terminally ill people to make that choice for themselves." [Tom Gordon](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-92AA7718-9E10-4833-9800-7471AF83EBE7), Liberal Democrat MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, said "we can give people a choice about the end of life and how they may see that end." [Liz Kendall](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-D0E04AEF-A214-47BE-A471-6A2723925F86), Labour MP for Leicester West, called it "the most scrutinised Bill I have seen in my 16 years in Parliament." [Siân Berry](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-11/debates/4C64BB03-23BC-41DF-8837-50F7BD082F9C/#contribution-C8AE499A-AED9-4B1F-9503-7D03648753E8), Green MP for Brighton Pavilion, urged members "to please vote for this Bill to continue."
