# MPs Warn Against Automatic Parental Rights for Surrogacy in Commons Debate

Members from across the House warned against sweeping away legal safeguards for surrogate mothers and children during a Commons debate on a petition calling for intended parents to be recognised as legal parents from birth in surrogacy arrangements, rather than having to apply for a parental order after the child is born.

The debate, opened by [Dave Robertson](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-07/debates/1DE05B16-B467-48D0-B6E7-6B7AD7F8B921/#contribution-988F3AEC-400F-4E73-9359-3A7544485295) (Labour, Lichfield) on behalf of the Petitions Committee, considered petition 763161, which was signed by 113,084 people. Under the current law, set out in the Human Fertilehood and Embryology Act 2008, the surrogate who gives birth is the legal mother until a court grants a parental order transferring parenthood to the intended parents, a process that cannot begin until six weeks after birth.

Eight MPs spoke in defence of retaining the existing safeguards, while seven members argued for reform or raised other concerns. Responding for the Government, [Dame Diana Johnson](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-07/debates/1DE05B16-B467-48D0-B6E7-6B7AD7F8B921/#contribution-C1C638B5-8256-4228-AF18-940E1E723411) said the Government "recognise the difficulties that intended parents may encounter when applying for a parental order" but that safeguarding assessments "are necessary to support the court's considerations."

The strongest warning came from [Jonathan Hinder](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-07/debates/1DE05B16-B467-48D0-B6E7-6B7AD7F8B921/#contribution-DA6C49F5-1139-4337-8BB6-27C34DAC052F) (Labour, Pendle and Clitheroe), who told the House: "Pregnant women are not factories, and babies are not goods to be ordered. I believe this is crossing an ethical line, where human life is treated as a business transaction." He cited the testimony of a surrogate mother, Marie Anne, who described being told after handing over her baby, "They had the baby. They were happy. They didn't need me anymore so they told me to go home," and who said "I have been diagnosed with complex PTSD…I have a deep fear of hospitals, children and babies…The damage done to me will never be repaired." Mr Hinder noted that "France, Germany, Spain and Italy have all banned surrogacy outright" and quoted President Macron's description of surrogacy as "not compatible with the dignity of women."

[Steve Yemm](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-07/debates/1DE05B16-B467-48D0-B6E7-6B7AD7F8B921/#contribution-0AC09DA4-FA0A-4E4D-98F3-C557FB770E14) (Labour, Mansfield) argued the six-week rule was essential: "Consent given before pregnancy cannot simply become irrevocable after childbirth." He said "the woman who carries and gives birth to a child is the legal mother" and concluded that "the six-week period after birth, the mother's consent and independent judicial oversight are not antiquated obstacles to modern families, but protections for women and children—important safeguards that I believe are worth keeping."

[Shivani Raja](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-07/debates/1DE05B16-B467-48D0-B6E7-6B7AD7F8B921/#contribution-8E870579-0B4E-40BA-B4DD-0E98EBDEAA41) (Conservative, Leicester East) said automatically recognising intended parents "could remove important safeguards at precisely the moment when a child is most vulnerable," citing a 2023 Court of Appeal case in which a surrogate mother won contact after a parental order was set aside. She concluded: "Compassion must never mean abandoning scrutiny, because when adults disagree, a child has to live with the consequences. That is why I cannot support automatic legal parenthood from birth."

[Rebecca Smith](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-07/debates/1DE05B16-B467-48D0-B6E7-6B7AD7F8B921/#contribution-17E905E2-9A04-4FF9-9187-8C9C19329C08) (Conservative, South West Devon) noted the UK is "an outlier in allowing any form of surrogacy at all," pointing out that it is "much more strictly limited, or even completely prohibited" in France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Sweden and Switzerland, and that a UN special rapporteur has "recommended the global abolition of surrogacy." She said typical reimbursements to surrogates "now reach as much as £25,000," calling that "a substantial sum, which should give us pause for thought."

[Tracy Gilbert](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-07/debates/1DE05B16-B467-48D0-B6E7-6B7AD7F8B921/#contribution-4C2DC75F-A5FC-4485-B3D7-25853C645973) (Labour, Edinburgh North and Leith) warned the Law Commission's proposals would "tip the balance of power away from the rights of the birth mother," asking "is it ever a free choice for a surrogate to enter into a surrogacy agreement?"

[Dr Neil Shastri-Hurst](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-07/debates/1DE05B16-B467-48D0-B6E7-6B7AD7F8B921/#contribution-5EEB8405-C487-4758-9EAE-AAF6BF258F2E) (Conservative, Solihull West and Shirley), a former member of the HFEA appeals panel, said any reform must "protect the child," "preserve the free and continuing consent of the woman who gives birth" and address "safeguarding concerns arising from the increase in international commercial surrogacy." [Jim Shannon](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-07/debates/1DE05B16-B467-48D0-B6E7-6B7AD7F8B921/#contribution-6F03F3DB-B821-41D8-9155-D18C2CA8A647) (DUP, Strangford) said "an important distinction must be made between the women who voluntarily agree to carry a child for someone they know...and the wholly exploitative system whereby a woman's womb becomes a service that can be bought," adding "I am deeply grieved by any idea that promotes fertility as something that can be bought and sold." [Phil Brickell](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-07/debates/1DE05B16-B467-48D0-B6E7-6B7AD7F8B921/#contribution-3A76514C-0334-482D-9E1C-30EA7955010A) (Labour, Bolton West) raised constituents' "repeated traumatic experiences" with an overseas surrogacy agency, asking whether it should be removed from the Government's official list pending review.

Those arguing for reform included Mr Robertson, who set out the Law Commission's 2023 proposal for a new pathway to legal parenthood from birth; [Josh Newbury](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-07/debates/1DE05B16-B467-48D0-B6E7-6B7AD7F8B921/#contribution-4A627378-85B7-437D-B061-73DB05181E25) (Labour, Cannock Chase), who asked whether "the law should leave their legal parenthood unresolved for months"; [Rachel Taylor](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-07/debates/1DE05B16-B467-48D0-B6E7-6B7AD7F8B921/#contribution-B285E41E-EE8A-4B4A-BFD5-AB8C4FED6A8C) (Labour, North Warwickshire and Bedworth), who said the current system "creates unnecessary stress and anguish"; [Jess Brown-Fuller](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-07/debates/1DE05B16-B467-48D0-B6E7-6B7AD7F8B921/#contribution-69FC68F0-6D30-4956-9FE1-8104316EA62B) (Liberal Democrat, Chichester), who pressed the Government for a formal response to the Law Commission report; and [John Milne](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-07/debates/1DE05B16-B467-48D0-B6E7-6B7AD7F8B921/#contribution-2BF5C4A2-D278-4B83-A7CB-9FC8B696B956) (Liberal Democrat, Horsham), who cited a constituent case and asked "does my hon. Friend agree that the Government should now respond to that report?" [Gordon McKee](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-09-07/debates/1DE05B16-B467-48D0-B6E7-6B7AD7F8B921/#contribution-C3618804-EA1D-4C13-8147-1037496F06B4) (Labour, Glasgow South) raised the additional cost of privately-funded assessments in Scotland.

Responding for the Government, Dame Diana Johnson said Ministers "recognise that surrogacy is a complex and sensitive policy issue where detail matters to all those involved," confirming that the Department "is looking into the allegations about My Surrogacy Journey" and would "consider whether it is appropriate for that company to remain on the gov.uk list of agencies." She said the Government "acknowledge those concerns and recognise the arguments on both sides" but gave no commitment to remove the parental order process, stating that "although the safeguarding assessments take time, they are necessary to support the court's considerations of parental order applications."
