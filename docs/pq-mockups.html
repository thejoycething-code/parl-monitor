Written questions: fuller data, four ways

Today the edition prints a capped list of five written questions as one-line entries with no asker, no department and no sense of how many were found. A sitting week typically matches 30 to 60, so most of what we gather is invisible. All four options below hyperlink every question to its page on parliament.uk and name the member who asked it. They differ in how they handle volume.

One constraint to note. Collapsible blocks work on the partner site and in the repo's markdown, but a Slack canvas renders a plainer markdown subset, so the "expandable" options need a fallback there: either the section stays flat with a cap and links out, or the full data lives on a linked page (option D). Every option below states its Slack behaviour.

A — Full table, grouped by issue area

Everything gathered, nothing hidden, sorted so the week's clusters are obvious. Honest and complete; the risk is a 47-row table dominating the edition when the rest of the week is quiet.

In Slack: works as-is — canvases render tables. Long weeks make for a long scroll.

D — Tight edition, complete companion page

The structural answer, and the only one that solves Slack properly. The edition and canvas keep the highlights; every single thing gathered lives on an internal page that regenerates each week, sortable and filterable, linked from the section heading. Also the natural home for everything else we collect and never print — background-scored items, discards, and the full ledger week.

In Slack: ideal — short canvas, one link to everything.

What each needs from the pipeline

  • All four: store the asking member and the answering department on the item row. Both are already in the API response and the member is already in the ledger; the item store just doesn't keep them, which is why today's lines can't name anyone.
  • A, B, C: remove the cap of five and render the fuller row.
  • C: a clustering step — cheapest version groups by shared matched term plus area, which would have found the Islamophobia cluster without any AI.
  • D: a new weekly page generator, and a decision about where it is hosted — behind the partner site's passphrase would be wrong, since this is internal data; it wants either the repo or a separate gated page.
