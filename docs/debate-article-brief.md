# Debate article: the brief

A news report of a parliamentary debate for our supporters and for pick-up by
friendly outlets. Modelled on Right To Life UK's debate reports (declarative
headline, news peg in the first paragraph, MPs introduced by party and seat, long
verbatim quotes, the Government's line, sources) but openly from our perspective:
the MPs who spoke for life, family and freedom are the story.

## Defaults (set 8 September 2026 on the surrogacy debate)

* **Length**: about 800 words. Write the full version first (2,000-2,500 words, one
  section per speaker) and keep it in the pack as `article.md`; the 800-word version
  is `article-800.md`.
* **Shape**: continuous prose, no subheadings, no organisation quote. Headline is
  declarative and names the House: "MPs from across the House speak out against
  surrogacy law change".
* **Lead** (three paragraphs): who spoke against what, with the parties named;
  the peg (petition number and signatures, or Bill and stage), what the law is now
  and what the proposal would change; the count of speakers each way and the
  Government's one-line answer.
* **Speakers**: party and constituency on first mention, name linked to the Hansard
  contribution. Order by strength of quote, not by speaking order. Long block
  quotes for the two or three strongest; one inline quote each for the rest.
  The front bench goes after the backbenchers who made the argument.
* **Quotations are Hansard wording, verbatim**, including Hansard's tidying. The
  video captions use the spoken words; the article uses the record. Never
  reconstruct a quote from a video transcript.
* **The other side**: one paragraph naming who argued for the change and their
  best point, stated fairly. The Minister's line in one paragraph, quoting the
  operative sentence ("not in a position to bring forward legislation").
* **Facts to verify live before sending**: petition number and count
  (`petition.parliament.uk/petitions/<id>.json`), Hansard link, the count of
  speakers each way from `checklist.md`.
* **Never**: invent a parliamentarian's name or words; attribute a statement to
  the organisation without sign-off; use a Hansard link you have not opened.
* **Sources footer**: Hansard debate link, petition or Bill link, recording.
