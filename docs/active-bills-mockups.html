Active bills — four views

The live business is the page's most timely content and currently its least distinguished: a one-line strip above the search, and a grey "NO VOTES YET" card that borrows the furniture of the historic vote cards. Four ways to make live Bills read as a different species. All data below is real, from the bills board as of 31 August.

The Order Paper panel

The strip grows into a real panel between the hero and the search — every live Bill, dated or not, each with a calendar leaf, the assisted-suicide Bill highlighted with the petition on its own row. One place answers "what is Parliament about to do on our issues", and the member cards stop carrying page-level news.

B

The live ticket

Page structure stays exactly as it is; the active Bill's card on each member page changes species: amber edge, LIVE marker with a pulse, the date set big on the right. Settled votes stay green/red/grey; live business is the only amber thing on the page, so the eye finds it before reading a word.

C

The countdown spine

The strip becomes a timeline: live Bills as nodes on a line, ordered by date, labelled by how soon — "in 4 days" does what "4 Sep 2026" doesn't. The assisted-suicide node is enlarged and tinted, with the petition. Reads left-to-right as "this Friday, next Friday, the autumn."

D

Live now / The record

A structural answer: the member card opens with an inverted LIVE NOW band — brand blue, unmissable — holding the active business, then a labelled divider hands over to "THE RECORD" and the historic cards. Live vs settled becomes the page's top-level grammar rather than a styling detail.
