## Step 1 — find the division(s), from the record not the rumour

    python3 tools/find_division_candidates.py 2026-09-11 2026-09-11

Expect one to three Commons divisions: possibly a **closure motion**,
possibly a **reasoned amendment**, then (if closure carries) **the
Second Reading question itself**. Treat each SEPARATELY.

## Step 2 — read what was actually put to the vote

The standing lesson (Lords inversion, 2026-08-31; ePrivacy fast-track,
2026-09-01): the meaning comes from the QUESTION, never the title.

- Second Reading question: "That the Bill be now read a second time."
- A reasoned amendment INVERTS the polarity: its question is to decline
  a second reading, so Aye = against the Bill. Do not reuse the 2R
  meanings for it.
- A closure motion is procedural but meaningful (the ePrivacy
  precedent): Aye = let the Bill reach its vote. Whether it gets a
  verdict is Christopher's call on the day.

## Step 4 — anchor test before the sign-off ping

Build locally (`python3 tools/make_vote_tracker.py` — `fetch_missing`
pulls and archives the new division by id automatically), then check

- **Danny Kruger** (Commons anti-AS anchor) must land GOOD VOTE.
- **Lauren Edwards** (the sponsor) must land BAD VOTE.

Either anchor on the wrong colour = the meaning or our_side is wrong;
stop and re-read the question.

## Contingencies

- **Talked out / closure fails**: no 2R division. Update the issue's
  `status:` line and board `next_key_date` when the new date is
  announced; the band keeps the Bill. Nothing is signed.
- **Passes on the nod** (no division): no member votes exist. Status
  line only; the "vote will appear here" promise updates to say what
  happened.
- **Multiple divisions** (reasoned amendment + 2R): one config entry
  each, each with its own question-grounded meanings, one sign-off
  message covering all.
- **Votes API slow**: the list endpoint lags occasionally; the division
  id also appears in the CommonsVotes site search. Do not build from
  press reports.
