The "last moved" indicator

The design problem to settle before the visual one. A placement can change for two completely different reasons: the member did something new, or we changed our minds. Tonight's rescore moved roughly 460 speeches from neutral to directional, and had this column existed it would have flashed "moved" beside hundreds of members who had done nothing at all. Any indicator that conflates the two is worse than no indicator, because it invents movement in Parliament out of our own housekeeping.

The fix is cheap: the weekly snapshot stores each member's placement and the evidence that decided it. If the deciding evidence is new, the member moved. If it is the same piece of evidence rescored, we moved. The treatments below all assume that distinction is being drawn.

1 — Board vocabulary (my recommendation)

Reuses the words the bills board already uses, so the edition speaks one language throughout: NEW, ▲ moved, no change. The from-and-to and the date carry the detail. Our own rescores get a separate, quieter marker so they are visible but never mistaken for a member changing position.

2 — From → to only

Terser: the transition alone, no vocabulary to learn. Reads well in a spreadsheet and survives being pasted into a Brief. Loses the distinction between a member moving and us reassessing unless a marker is added anyway, which is how it collapses back into option 1.

3 — Age of the placement

Answers a different and arguably better question: not "did they move" but "how old is this judgement". A placement resting on a 2020 speech and one resting on last month's vote look identical on every other treatment. Cheap to compute from the deciding evidence alone, and it needs no snapshot table at all — so it could ship this week.

Not mutually exclusive with 1: "Moved" says whether it changed, "Based on" says how much to trust it. Together they are most of what a confidence marker would tell you.

4 — Placement trail

The whole history in one cell, most recent last. Genuinely informative for a member who has drifted over years, and useless for the 600 who have never moved. Worth holding for the per-member profile rather than the sheet, where 649 mostly-flat trails would be noise.

What I would do

Option 1 for the sheet, option 3 alongside it, option 4 on the profile page. Option 1 keeps one movement vocabulary across the whole product, and its separate "reassessed" marker is the thing that stops the column lying. Option 3 costs almost nothing, needs no snapshot table, and could ship immediately — and between them they answer both questions a campaigner has: has this changed, and how much should I trust it.

One consequence worth accepting up front: like the bills board, the first sheet with this column is a baseline. Every row reads NEW until there are two snapshots to compare, so the column earns its place in week two, not week one.
