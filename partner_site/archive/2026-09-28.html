What ministers were asked - and what they said

24 questions on our issues, answered in 22 replies. 10 replies, covering 12 questions, added nothing new and are listed at the end.

Migration (5 questions)

Immigration - Mr Louie French (Conservative, Old Bexley and Sidcup) asked Home Office, answered 21 Sep

*Asked:* what she defines as immigration pull factors to the United Kingdom; and what steps she is taking to remove those pull factors.

*Answered:* The Government considers immigration pull factors to be aspects of the UK's asylum and immigration system that may incentivise irregular migration to the United Kingdom. Through the Restoring Order and Control Policy Statement and the Immigration and Asylum Bill, introduced on 30 June 2026 the Government is reforming the asylum...

*Why it matters:* Sets out Government's framing of migration pull factors and legislative response, relevant to border integrity and illegal migration policy.

Deportation - Mr Louie French (Conservative, Old Bexley and Sidcup) asked Home Office, answered 21 Sep

*Asked:* what steps she is taking to ensure that those who have had asylum claims rejected are removed from the United Kingdom; and what percentage of rejected claimants have been removed from the UK since July 2024.

*Answered:* Since the July 2024 election, asylum-related returns have reached their highest level since 2010. The monthly breakdown in asylum related returns since July 2024 is published in the ad-hoc transparency release, which can be found here: Returns from the UK between 1 July 2024 and 31 July 2026 - GOV.UK...

*Why it matters:* Claims record asylum returns since 2024, a concrete figure bearing on border control and illegal migration enforcement.

Victims: Women - Joy Morrissey (Conservative, Beaconsfield) asked Home Office, answered 22 Sep

*Asked:* what assessment she has made of whether the age profile of female victims of crimes allegedly committed by asylum seekers differs from that of female victims of comparable offences committed by the wider population.

*Answered:* The Home Office has not made an assessment of whether the age profile of female victims of crimes allegedly committed by asylum seekers differs from that of female victims of comparable offences committed by the wider population. All criminal offences are investigated by the police and prosecuted independently where appropriate.

*Why it matters:* Government admits no assessment made of asylum-linked crime victim demographics, leaving community cohesion concerns unaddressed and unanswered.

Gender Based Violence: Undocumented Migrants - Joy Morrissey (Conservative, Beaconsfield) asked Home Office, answered 22 Sep

*Asked:* what estimate she has made of the number of women and girls who have been victims of violence or sexual offences allegedly committed by people who entered the UK illegally in small boats in each of the last...

*Answered:* The Home Office does not centrally hold data that would enable an estimate to be made of the number of women and girls who have been victims of sexual offences allegedly committed by people who entered the UK illegally by small boat.

*Why it matters:* No data held on victims of crimes by illegal small-boat arrivals limits public understanding of migration's link to violence against women.

Gender Based Violence: Undocumented Migrants - Joy Morrissey (Conservative, Beaconsfield) asked Home Office, answered 24 Sep

*Asked:* what data her Department holds on the number of women and girls who have been victims of crimes committed by asylum seekers in each of the last five years, broken down by age of victim and type of...

*Answered:* The Home Office does not centrally hold data on the number of women and girls who have been victims of crimes committed by asylum seekers, broken down by the age of the victim and type of offence.

*Why it matters:* Absence of data on crimes by asylum seekers against women and girls hampers evidence-based debate on migration and community safety.

Free speech, privacy and civil liberties (3 questions)

Internet: Age Assurance - Bell Ribeiro-Addy (Labour, Clapham and Brixton Hill) asked Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport, answered 21 Sep

*Asked:* what assessment her Department has made of the potential merits of using device or operating-system-level age signalling to reduce the need for individual websites to collect identity documents or biometric data for the purposes of age verification under...

*Answered:* We recognise the potential merits of safety protections at device-level, including age assurance. The Government is engaging with operating system providers and device manufacturers to make their products safer for children, including through technology to prevent children from taking, sharing or viewing nude imagery.

*Why it matters:* Device-level age assurance could reduce invasive ID checks; government engagement here shapes how online safety rules affect privacy and free expression.

Anti-Muslim Hatred/Islamophobia Definition Working Group - Lord Jackson of Peterborough (Conservative, Life peer) asked Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government, answered 21 Sep

*Asked:* further to the Written Answer by Baroness Blake of Leeds on 1 September (HL2208), were written submissions from organisations subject to the department’s policy of non-engagement substantively considered or disregarded by the working group.

*Answered:* No organisations subject to the Government's policy of non-engagement were engaged by the Definition Working Group.

*Why it matters:* Confirms organisations under a government non-engagement policy were excluded from the definition working group, raising concerns about viewpoint exclusion in policymaking.

Anti-Muslim Hostility - Lord Jackson of Peterborough (Conservative, Life peer) asked Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government, answered 22 Sep

*Asked:* further to the Written Answer by Baroness Smith of Malvern on 16 July (HL1590), whether criticism is Islamist practices is deemed to fall within the definition of racism.

*Answered:* Matters relating to discrimination, harassment and hate crime are governed by legislation, and the Government recognises the fundamental right to freedom of expression within the law. The Government has adopted a non-statutory definition of anti-Muslim hostility.

*Why it matters:* Confirms criticism of Islamist practices is not deemed racism, an important clarification protecting free speech on religion from being conflated with hate.

Sex based rights (3 questions)

Gender Based Violence: Undocumented Migrants - Joy Morrissey (Conservative, Beaconsfield) asked Home Office, answered 22 Sep

*Asked:* what assessment she has made of the prevalence of violence against women and girls among crimes committed by people who are in the UK without legal immigration status.

*Answered:* Asylum seekers who engage in antisocial or criminal behaviour will be subject to the same legal consequences as any other individual residing in the country and will have their immigration status reviewed, if convicted.

*Why it matters:* Government sidesteps question on VAWG prevalence among undocumented migrants, restating general enforcement and strategy without new data.

Social Media: Children - Sarah Green (Liberal Democrat, Chesham and Amersham) asked Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport, answered 23 Sep

*Asked:* what assessment she has made of the potential risk of the proposed under-16 social media ban increasing children's exposure to misogynist and extremist rhetoric through migration to unregulated online spaces; and what steps her Department is taking to...

*Answered:* Tackling violence against women and girls remains a top priority for this government. The banning of social media companies from offering their services to under-16s will help provide additional protections for children.

*Why it matters:* Raises concern that under-16 social media bans could push children toward unregulated extremist spaces, affecting child safety policy design.

Civil Servants: Transgender People - Mike Wood (Conservative, Kingswinford and South Staffordshire) asked Cabinet Office, answered 23 Sep

*Asked:* pursuant to the answer of 12 June 2026 to Question 4166 on Equality: Gender and Intersex, whether the new guidance changes the 13 weeks of paid special leave for transgender staff undergoing gender reassignment.

*Answered:* The model Special Leave suite of products was updated in May 2026 to align with the provision for all other employees, as set out in the model Policy and Guidance: Supporting Trans and Non-binary employees and Supporting Attendance Policy and Guidance.

*Why it matters:* Removal of enhanced special leave for gender reassignment signals a policy shift away from bespoke trans workplace provisions, relevant to gender ideology in institutions.

Parental rights education (1 question)

Offences against Children - Sammy Wilson (Democratic Unionist Party, East Antrim) asked Home Office, answered 22 Sep

*Asked:* how many individuals identified as members of organised grooming gangs have been charged with and convicted of rape offences in the United Kingdom in each of the last ten years.

*Answered:* The Home Office does not hold the information requested. Information on criminal offences and criminal justice outcomes is not routinely recorded or published in a way that identifies whether an offender was a member of an organised grooming gang.

*Why it matters:* Lack of data on grooming gang offenders' convictions limits scrutiny of organised abuse linked to community cohesion concerns.
