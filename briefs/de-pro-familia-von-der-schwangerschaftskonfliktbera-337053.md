### What are we asking for in the petition?

Members of the German Bundestag must vote to remove pro familia from the list of organisations permitted to carry out Schwangerschaftskonfliktberatung, the mandatory counselling required by law before a legal abortion can proceed. This is owed to every mother in crisis and every unborn child whose life still hangs on that conversation: the law was written to guarantee a neutral voice for the child at the most vulnerable moment of a pregnancy, and a provider bound up with networks that facilitate abortion access cannot give that voice honestly. MPs on the Familienausschuss, now examining this motion after its referral, hold the power to correct this conflict of interest and restore the counselling system to the purpose it was always meant to serve: protecting unborn life, not rubber stamping a decision already made.

### What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?

Counselling in name only: pro familia is one of Germany's largest issuers of the certificate required before a legal termination, yet the organisation sits close to networks that also facilitate access to abortion, meaning the body entrusted with protecting the unborn child may have no real interest in doing so.

The law's promise betrayed: Germany's counselling requirement exists precisely to give the unborn child a voice and the mother a genuine chance to choose life before an irreversible step is taken. When the counsellor is not neutral, that legal safeguard becomes a formality rather than a protection.

Vulnerable women deceived: a mother facing a crisis pregnancy is told she will receive impartial guidance. Instead she may be steered by an organisation whose institutional interests do not align with hers or her child's.

Unborn children pay the price: every conflict of interest inside that counselling room is one fewer genuine chance for a child to be protected before a decision that cannot be undone.

Silence keeps the status quo: without this motion succeeding, pro familia continues in its certifying role indefinitely, and a system that fails both mothers and unborn children carries on unchallenged.

Public trust hollowed out: Germans are told this counselling law exists to protect life. Every day pro familia remains an approved provider, that promise rings a little less true.

### What are some arguments supporting our point of view?

A measurable conflict of interest: pro familia's institutional links to organisations that facilitate abortion access compromise its ability to offer counselling that is genuinely unbiased. [VERIFY]

The law exists to protect, not to process: Schwangerschaftskonfliktberatung was designed to ensure every woman considers real alternatives to abortion with a truly neutral adviser, not to rubber stamp a predetermined outcome.

Certification without neutrality protects nobody: the certificate is meant to prove that meaningful counselling took place. If the counsellor's institutional loyalties point one way, the certificate becomes paperwork, not protection.

Mothers deserve honest options: genuine crisis pregnancy counselling should set out every path open to a woman, including real support to keep her child, not quietly funnel her toward one outcome.

There is precedent for excluding conflicted providers: gatekeeping standards elsewhere require that certifying bodies have no institutional stake in the outcome they certify.

Taxpayers are owed accountability: public funding underwrites this counselling system, and taxpayers are entitled to know it actually serves the life protecting purpose it was built for.

This motion offers a concrete fix, not a vague pledge: excluding a named conflicted provider addresses the problem directly rather than leaving it to future promises.

Other countries require independent providers before an abortion decision is finalised, recognising that neutrality cannot coexist with institutional self interest. [VERIFY]

### Why is it urgent that we take action now?

This motion has just been referred to the Familienausschuss for committee scrutiny. That referral is the moment decisions get made quietly, before most people are even aware a vote is coming.

Once a committee reports back and the Bundestag votes, the counselling framework that follows locks in for years. Reversing a settled arrangement is far harder than shaping it now, while the motion is still being weighed.

This is the test of a promise Germany made when it wrote the counselling requirement into law: that no unborn child would face a termination decision without a mother first receiving a truly neutral hearing for the alternative of life. This motion asks whether that promise still means anything.

If supporters of life stay silent now, while the motion sits in committee, MPs will read that silence as consent to leave a conflicted provider in place. This is the window. It will not stay open long.

### Why would they listen to us?

The AfD has already brought this motion forward and secured its referral to committee, meaning the question is live and MPs are actively weighing it right now, not deciding whether to consider it at all.

Committee stage is exactly the point where public pressure counts most, before positions harden into a party line ahead of a floor vote. MPs on family and life issues have shifted committee outcomes before when constituents made clear volumes of concern known. [VERIFY]

Members who represent conservative and faith communities have a direct incentive to be seen defending the neutrality of counselling law, since it touches values their own voters care about deeply.

### Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:

If the Familienausschuss shelves or rejects this motion, pro familia continues to issue counselling certificates unchanged. Mothers in crisis keep receiving guidance from a provider tied to the very networks that offer abortion access, and unborn children keep losing the protection the counselling law was written to give them.

### Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:

The Bundestag votes to remove pro familia from the list of approved counselling providers, restoring genuine neutrality to the process. Every mother facing a crisis pregnancy receives a fair, unconflicted hearing for the choice of life, and every unborn child is given the chance the law always promised: to be considered, not overlooked.

### Ideas for eventual Offline Actions

Organise prayer vigils outside pregnancy counselling centres to hold the moment in public witness. Write personal letters to members of the Familienausschuss explaining why neutral counselling matters to your family. Share testimonies from women who felt pressured rather than supported during crisis pregnancy counselling. Place inserts in church bulletins explaining the motion and asking congregants to write to their MP. Run local petition signing drives outside town halls and church halls to show visible public support before the committee reports back.
