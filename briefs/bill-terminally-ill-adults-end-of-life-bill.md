**What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?**

This Bill asks Parliament to redefine the relationship between doctor and patient, and between the state and the vulnerable, by opening the door to state-sanctioned killing of the terminally ill. The elderly, the disabled, the depressed and the poor are always the ones put at risk when 'choice' becomes policy. Real choice means being cared for until the natural end, not being offered death as the cheaper option.

## Prepare phase prompts

**What are some arguments supporting our point of view?**

- No safeguard has ever proven robust enough to stop 'assisted dying' laws widening over time, as seen in every jurisdiction that has introduced them.
- Doctors are trained to heal, not to end life. Asking the medical profession to facilitate death corrodes trust between patient and physician.
- Palliative care in the UK remains underfunded. The answer to suffering is better care, not a quicker death.
- Disabled people's organisations have repeatedly warned that assisted dying laws send the message that some lives are less worth living.
- Pressure on the vulnerable, whether financial, emotional or from family, cannot be legislated away once death becomes a legal 'treatment' option.

**Why is it urgent that we take action now?**

This Bill is at 2nd reading in the Commons and the next key date is 11 September 2026. The debate is already intense, with 345 debate contributions logged in Parliament over the last six months. MPs are weighing this now. Every letter, every voice, every prayer matters before this vote is taken.

**Why would they listen to us?**

MPs must listen because 2nd reading is where they set the direction of the whole Bill. A strong, visible wave of constituent opposition ahead of 11 September can give wavering MPs the confidence and the cover to vote no. This is a free vote on a matter of conscience, and conscience responds to conviction.

**Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:**

If this Bill passes 2nd reading and progresses, Britain moves a decisive step closer to legalising assisted death. Vulnerable people, the elderly, the disabled and those in despair could be steered towards ending their lives rather than receiving the care and support they deserve. Once this line is crossed, history shows the boundaries only expand.

**Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:**

MPs vote down the Bill at 2nd reading, or it is so significantly checked that the principle of assisted killing is rejected outright. Parliament instead redirects attention and funding towards proper palliative and end of life care, affirming that every life, however fragile, is worth protecting and caring for to its natural end.
