**What are we asking for in the petition?**

We are asking supporters to submit evidence to the House of Lords Communications and Digital Committee inquiry into the Online Safety Act before written evidence closes on 7 September, demanding that any review recognise how the Act is being used to police lawful speech, and that Parliament build in real safeguards for freedom of expression, particularly for Christians, pro-life advocates and family campaigners speaking online.

**Why would they listen to us?**

This is a formal House of Lords Committee inquiry actively soliciting written evidence, meaning peers are required to consider submissions from the public and organisations, not just industry and regulators. The sheer volume of parliamentary activity on this issue in the last six months, 214 debate contributions, 94 written questions and 4 motions with 45 signatures, shows peers and MPs are already engaged and looking for real evidence of impact to inform their scrutiny.

**What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?**

The Online Safety Act was sold as protection for children. In practice it hands platforms and regulators sweeping powers to judge what speech is acceptable, with vague and shifting definitions of 'harm'. Lawful opinions on life, marriage and family are increasingly treated as dangerous simply because they are unfashionable. Ordinary citizens, churches and campaign groups face removal, restriction or silence online for saying things that are perfectly legal to say on the street.

## Prepare phase prompts

**What are some arguments supporting our point of view?**

- Free speech is not a privilege granted by platforms or regulators, it is the foundation of a free society and must be defended even when the speech is unpopular.
- Vague statutory language around 'harmful' content invites platforms to over-censor rather than risk regulatory penalties, so lawful conservative and faith-based views are the first casualties.
- The Act's enforcement architecture creates strong incentives for platforms to remove content pre-emptively, meaning legal speech is chilled long before any court or regulator rules on it.
- This Lords inquiry is a genuine, formal opportunity to get evidence of these harms on the parliamentary record before the Act becomes further entrenched in practice.
- The scale of parliamentary attention on this issue, over 200 debate contributions and 94 written questions in six months, shows this is a live and contested area of policy, not a settled matter.

**Why is it urgent that we take action now?**

Written evidence to this inquiry closes at 5pm on 7 September 2026. This is a fixed, hard deadline. Once it passes, the Committee moves to drafting its report and the window for citizen and campaign evidence to shape that report closes with it. The Online Safety Act is already in force and already affecting what people can say online today, so every week without pushback on the record is a week the status quo goes unchallenged.

**Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:**

The Committee's report endorses the current approach with no meaningful safeguards for free expression. Platforms continue to treat lawful pro-life, pro-family and Christian speech as high-risk content to be removed or restricted, and this becomes further normalised and embedded as the Act matures, with no parliamentary record challenging it.

**Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:**

The Committee's report explicitly documents the chilling effect the Act has had on lawful speech and recommends clearer statutory protections for freedom of expression, tighter definitions of harm, and greater transparency and accountability for platform takedowns. Families, churches and campaigners can speak about life, marriage and faith online without fear of arbitrary removal.
