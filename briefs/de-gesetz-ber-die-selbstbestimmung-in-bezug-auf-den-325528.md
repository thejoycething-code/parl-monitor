### What are we asking for in the petition?

We call on the members of the German Bundestag, and in particular the Committee on Legal Affairs and Consumer Protection now considering this motion, to vote to repeal the Selbstbestimmungsgesetz and restore a legal framework in which a person's registered sex can only be changed through a proper, verifiable process, not a mere declaration at a registry office. Parliament owes every woman who relies on a single-sex refuge, every girl who competes on a level playing field, and every vulnerable young person wrestling with their identity, a law that protects rather than erases the category of "woman" in law. A signature should never be allowed to override biology, and lawmakers who once promised to protect the vulnerable now have the chance, and the duty, to make that promise real.

### What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?

A signature can now overrule biology: since the self-ID law took effect, any adult can change their legal sex by a simple declaration, with no medical evidence, no waiting period and no independent check, turning a matter of fact into a matter of paperwork.

Women's spaces are no longer safe by law: refuges, changing rooms and prisons that were built on the presumption of single-sex safety can now be entered by anyone holding the correct documentation, regardless of their body, leaving women and girls with no legal ground to object.

Vulnerable young people are being pushed toward irreversible declarations: teenagers going through the confusion and pain of adolescence are being told that a legal change of sex is a simple administrative step, when in truth it can set them on a path that is very hard to undo.

Predators can exploit the loophole: a law with no safeguards does not merely inconvenience institutions, it hands bad actors a legal shield to enter spaces reserved for women and children, and the Bundestag has been warned of this risk.

Honest debate is being treated as prejudice: MPs and citizens who raise these concerns are frequently dismissed as bigots rather than engaged on the substance, which stifles the very scrutiny that good law-making requires.

The evidence from other countries is being ignored: jurisdictions that rushed into self-ID have since had to review or reverse course after real harm came to light, yet Germany's law remains in place as though those lessons do not apply.

### What are some arguments supporting our point of view?

Sex is a material fact, not a feeling: the law that protects women in refuges, hospital wards and prisons exists because male and female bodies differ in ways that matter for safety and dignity, and no declaration changes that.

Self-ID removes essential safeguards: repealing the law would restore basic checks, such as evidence and process, before a change with such serious consequences is made official.

Women's sport depends on sex-based categories: fairness in competition collapses the moment legal sex can be self-declared, and female athletes have already paid the price elsewhere.

Prisons must classify by sex, not paperwork: placing male-bodied prisoners in female estates on the strength of a declaration has already produced dangerous outcomes in other countries, and Germany should not repeat that mistake.

Children deserve time, not a rubber stamp: young people questioning their identity need careful, supportive guidance, not a legal system that treats a life-altering change as routine paperwork.

Detransitioners' experience is being ignored: growing numbers of people who once sought a change of legal sex have since regretted the decision, evidence that the current law's lack of safeguards causes real and lasting harm.

Data on sex matters for medicine and safety: accurate records of biological sex are essential for healthcare, crime statistics and safeguarding, and self-ID corrupts that data at the source.

Other nations are already retreating from this model: the wider evidence base, including reviews conducted elsewhere, has moved toward caution, and Germany's Bundestag should not be the last to notice.

The rule of law demands clarity, not confusion: a legal category as consequential as sex cannot rest on a declaration alone if the law is to remain coherent and enforceable.

### Why is it urgent that we take action now?

This motion is currently sitting with the Committee on Legal Affairs and Consumer Protection, with no fixed date yet set for it to return to the floor of the Bundestag, which means the window to make MPs feel the weight of public concern is open now, before the committee's recommendation is settled.

Every month the self-ID law remains untouched, more institutions, from refuges to sports federations to schools, are forced to rebuild their practices around it, and the longer that embedding continues, the harder any future correction becomes.

Germany's lawmakers once promised that reform would never come at the expense of women's and children's safety. This motion is the test of whether that promise still means anything, or whether it was simply words spoken to ease a controversial law through.

If supporters stay silent while this motion is debated, MPs will read that silence as consent, concluding that the public has made its peace with a law that lets biology be overridden by declaration. That is not a message we can afford to send.

### Why would they listen to us?

MPs from across the political spectrum, not only the AfD, have voiced concern in recent debates about safeguarding gaps and the exploitation of loopholes, showing that this is not a fringe worry but one shared more widely than the government's framing suggests. A visible, sustained public voice gives those MPs the confidence and the cover to support the motion rather than let it quietly die in committee.

### Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:

If the Bundestag lets this motion fail without serious debate, the self-ID law will remain fully in force, meaning any adult can continue to change their legal sex by simple declaration, women's refuges and prisons will continue to face entries they cannot lawfully refuse, and vulnerable young people will keep being told that a life-altering legal change is a routine formality rather than a decision deserving real safeguards.

### Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:

If the Bundestag votes to repeal the Selbstbestimmungsgesetz, Germany will restore a legal system in which changing one's registered sex requires genuine evidence and process, women's refuges, changing rooms and prisons will regain the legal footing to remain single-sex, women's sport will have firmer ground to defend fair categories, and young people wrestling with their identity will be met with care and caution rather than a rubber stamp.

### Ideas for eventual Offline Actions

Organise a briefing pack for sympathetic MPs ahead of the committee's report, summarising safeguarding incidents and detransitioner testimony for use in floor speeches. Coordinate a joint letter from women's groups, sports bodies and child safeguarding organisations calling for repeal. Hold a public vigil or press event outside the Bundestag on a committee sitting day, featuring women whose safety has been affected by the current law. Distribute door-drop leaflets in constituencies of undecided MPs explaining the motion in plain terms.
