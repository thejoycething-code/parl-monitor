**What are we asking for in the petition?**

The Members of the Committee now scrutinising the Child-like Sexual Abuse Dolls (Offences) Bill must complete their work without delay and report the Bill back to the Commons intact, so that manufacturing, importing, selling and possessing dolls designed to resemble children for sexual purposes becomes, at last, a specific criminal offence. Parliament owes this to every family in this country: the duty to close a loophole that currently allows objects built to simulate child abuse to sit legally in someone's home. Every week this Bill drifts without a firm date for its next stage is a week that duty goes unmet.

This Bill already has a named sponsor willing to put her name to it and has drawn genuine parliamentary attention, with dozens of debates and questions raised around it in recent months. **What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?**

No specific offence currently exists: right now, a person can manufacture, import or possess a doll built to look like a child, for the express purpose of simulating sexual abuse, and face no dedicated criminal charge for the object itself. That is not a technicality. It is a gap predators can and do exploit.

Delay is not neutral: the Bill has reached Committee stage in the Commons, but with no fixed date for its next step, it can sit indefinitely while the law that would protect children stays silent. Every day without a firm date is another day these dolls remain legally available.

Parental trust is undermined: parents have every right to expect that the state treats the sexualisation of childhood as intolerable. A legal grey area around these dolls tells families that protecting their children from being reduced to objects of abuse is optional, not owed.

Normalisation precedes harm: these dolls exist to rehearse the abuse of a child. Treating that as a private matter, rather than a criminal one, risks entrenching the idea that a child's likeness can be used this way without consequence.

Cross-party momentum could be squandered: the Bill was brought forward with sponsor support and has drawn real parliamentary engagement. To let it stall now, after that effort, would be a betrayal of the children it was written to protect.

## Prepare phase prompts

**What are some arguments supporting our point of view?**

These dolls exist for one purpose: to simulate the sexual abuse of a child. There is no innocent use, no ambiguous middle ground. Any argument that treats them as harmless novelty items ignores what they are built to represent.

They normalise the unthinkable: possessing an object that rehearses child abuse desensitises the user to the idea of a real child as a victim. That is precisely the pathway child protection experts warn against.

Parents have a right to a state that acts: families entrust Parliament with the basic duty of keeping predatory behaviour illegal. A law that names and criminalises these dolls is the state fulfilling that trust, not exceeding it.

The current gap in the law is indefensible: without a specific offence, prosecutors and police are left improvising with laws never designed for this purpose. A dedicated offence gives them the clear tool they need.

This is a matter of child protection, not free expression: no one's liberty is diminished by removing from legal commerce an object whose entire design purpose is to simulate the abuse of a child.

Cross-party engagement shows this can pass: with a named sponsor and real activity around the Bill, including debate and question time in Parliament, there is a genuine chance to see this through if the pressure holds.

Other jurisdictions have already acted: the direction of travel internationally is toward criminalising these objects outright, and the United Kingdom should not lag behind in protecting its own children.

Silence in Committee is not a safe default: bills can and do quietly stall at this stage when public attention moves elsewhere. Sustained pressure now is what keeps this one moving toward Royal Assent.

**Why is it urgent that we take action now?**

The Bill sits at Committee stage in the Commons right now, with no confirmed date for its next step. That uncertainty is itself the danger: bills without a fixed timetable can drift for months, losing momentum while the objects they would ban remain perfectly legal to own.

Once a Bill like this stalls in Committee, it becomes far harder to revive. Parliamentary time is scarce, and a Bill that loses its place in the queue can wait indefinitely for a slot that never comes, even with cross-party goodwill behind it.

This is the test of whether Parliament's stated commitment to child protection is more than words. A sponsor has brought this Bill forward in good faith, and MPs across parties have engaged with it through debate and question time. Now is the moment that commitment is proven or quietly abandoned.

If supporters go quiet now, that silence will be read in Westminster as a lack of public concern, and a Bill this important will slip down the list of priorities. It must not be allowed to.

**Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:**

If Parliament allows this Bill to stall in Committee, dolls built to simulate the sexual abuse of a child remain entirely legal to manufacture, import and possess in this country. Predators keep a tool that normalises the very harm our laws exist to prevent, and every family is left less protected than they were promised.

**Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:**

Parliament completes Committee stage without delay and carries the Bill through to become law, making it a specific criminal offence to manufacture, import, sell or possess a child-like sexual abuse doll. Children gain a protection the law has been missing, and families can trust that the state treats the sexualisation of childhood as the crime it is.
