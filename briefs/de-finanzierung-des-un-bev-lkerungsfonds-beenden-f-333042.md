### What are we asking for in the petition?

The German Bundestag must vote to end all federal funding to the UN Population Fund, because every euro handed to UNFPA is a euro that helps fund and normalise abortion access across the developing world. Members of the Bundestag now have this motion before them in committee, and they owe the German taxpayer, and the unborn children whose lives are treated as a line item in a UN budget, a clean and final break from this funding stream. Germany has no duty to bankroll an agency whose mission runs directly against the protection of life from conception, and the Bundestag must say so plainly, not quietly let the funding continue by default while the motion sits unresolved.

### What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?

German taxpayers are funding a global abortion agenda without their consent: money collected from ordinary families across Germany is funnelled through UNFPA into programmes that treat abortion as basic healthcare, with no vote of the German people ever asked on this use of their money.

Unborn children are the silent casualties of a funding line few citizens ever scrutinise: while parliament debates budgets in the abstract, UNFPA's programmes translate directly into abortions performed in countries with far weaker protections for life than Germany itself claims to uphold.

A motion to stop this is being quietly processed rather than urgently championed: the fact that this proposal has only reached "referred to committee" status, with no public reckoning demanded of it, shows how easily this funding could simply continue by inertia.

Germany's moral standing on the sanctity of life is undermined by its own chequebook: a country that debates the protection of the unborn at home cannot credibly claim to value life while exporting abortion promotion abroad through the UN system.

Silence from most parties leaves the field open to those who never intended to challenge this funding: without pressure now, this motion risks dying quietly in committee, and the funding will simply resume as though no objection was ever raised.

### What are some arguments supporting our point of view?

UNFPA funding is not neutral humanitarian aid: the fund has a long record of promoting abortion access as part of its reproductive health programming, meaning German contributions are never merely for maternal care or family planning in the abstract.

Germany's own constitution protects the dignity of human life: a state that recognises the value of life within its borders has no principled basis for funding an international body that treats the ending of life abroad as a health service.

Taxpayer money should never be spent without transparency about where it truly goes: most German citizens have no idea their taxes underwrite UNFPA's global operations, let alone the specific programmes those funds support.

Ending this funding would send a clear signal that Germany's pro-life commitments are more than rhetoric: words in the Bundestag chamber mean little if the money keeps flowing to organisations working against those very commitments.

Other nations have already withdrawn or restricted UNFPA funding over these concerns: Germany would not be acting alone or radically, but joining a growing recognition that this fund's activities warrant serious scrutiny.

The AfD motion, whatever one thinks of its origin, raises a question every party should be willing to answer: does the Bundestag support ending abortion-linked funding, or does it prefer to avoid the question by letting the motion languish?

Committee referral is not a resolution, it is an opportunity: the motion's current stage means the decision is still open, and public pressure now can shape how seriously it is examined rather than quietly shelved.

### Why is it urgent that we take action now?

This motion sits before the responsible Bundestag committee right now, referred rather than resolved, which means the window to influence its outcome is open today but will not stay open indefinitely.

Once a motion like this is allowed to stall in committee without pressure, it tends to disappear from the agenda entirely, and the funding it challenges simply continues unchanged, year after year, without ever facing a real vote or public debate.

Germany has never made an explicit public promise to end this funding, but every party that claims to respect the sanctity of life carries an implicit duty to examine exactly where taxpayer money goes and to act when it funds programmes that work against that principle. This motion is the test of whether that duty is honoured or quietly set aside.

If supporters stay silent now, the Bundestag will read that silence as permission to let the funding continue as normal. A motion that never generates public attention is a motion easily buried, and the cost of that silence is paid by the unborn children UNFPA's programmes affect every single day this funding continues.

### Why would they listen to us?

Members of the Bundestag respond to visible public pressure on motions still open in committee, precisely because the outcome is not yet fixed and a wave of constituent contact can shift how seriously a motion is examined. Parties weighing whether to support or block this proposal will take note if large numbers of citizens make clear that funding an abortion-promoting UN agency is not something they are willing to fund quietly through their taxes.

### Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:

If the Bundestag lets this motion stall in committee, German taxpayer funds will continue flowing to UNFPA without challenge, and the programmes that fund and normalise abortion abroad will carry on exactly as before, with no accountability and no public debate about whether Germany should be underwriting them at all.

### Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:

If the Bundestag acts on this motion, Germany ends its financial support for UNFPA, taxpayer money is redirected away from abortion-promoting programmes, and Germany takes a clear, public stand that protecting life comes before funding a UN agenda that treats abortion as routine healthcare.

### Ideas for eventual Offline Actions

Organise a letter-writing drive to Bundestag committee members currently reviewing the motion, asking supporters to state plainly that they do not want their taxes funding UNFPA. Coordinate a small delegation to deliver signed petitions directly to the committee office in Berlin. Encourage churches and pro-life groups to host short information evenings explaining what UNFPA funding actually pays for, using this motion as the concrete example. Distribute simple one-page flyers outside town halls summarising the funding figures and the moral stakes, with a QR code linking to the petition.
