### What are we asking for in the petition?

We are asking the Bundesrat to refuse to wave through the Fifth Act amending the "Mother and Child – Protection of Unborn Life" Foundation without full, public scrutiny of every change to its funding and scope. This Foundation exists because Germany once promised that no woman facing a crisis pregnancy would be left without practical help, and that no unborn child's life would be treated as expendable for want of support. The Bundesrat holds the next stage of that promise in its hands. Before it lets this bill pass into law, it owes mothers, families and unborn children a transparent accounting of exactly what is changing, why, and who stands to lose if the Foundation's reach is narrowed.

### What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?

A vital lifeline could be quietly rewritten: The Mother and Child Foundation is one of the few concrete supports offered to women choosing life for their unborn child, and any amendment to its founding law can alter who it helps and how much it can give, often without the public even noticing.

Mothers in crisis could be left with less, not more: If funding or eligibility rules are narrowed under this amendment, it is the woman facing an unplanned pregnancy in genuine hardship who pays the price, at the exact moment she most needs practical help to choose her child's life over abortion.

The unborn are being spoken for without being heard: A Foundation created explicitly to protect unborn life is being reshaped through a legislative process that, as things stand, has involved no public debate, no committee hearings on record, and no stated commitments about what will not be cut.

Transparency has been bypassed at the first hurdle: The bill has been sent to the Bundesrat but has not yet been deliberated there. That means the substantive detail of what this amendment actually does to the Foundation's protections is not yet public knowledge, and families who rely on it are being asked to trust a process they cannot see.

Silence now becomes consent later: Once this amendment clears the Bundesrat and is adopted, whatever changes it makes to the Foundation's scope become the new legal baseline. There will be no easy undoing of a weakened Foundation once it is law.

### What are some arguments supporting our point of view?

A promise made in law deserves to be kept in practice: The Mother and Child Foundation was established as Germany's concrete answer to abortion, offering real help instead of an easy choice. Any amendment must be measured against whether it strengthens or quietly erodes that answer.

Crisis pregnancy support saves lives, not just headlines: Every woman who receives practical, financial or material help through this Foundation is a woman given a genuine alternative to abortion. Weakening that support removes a real option, not an abstract one.

Vague amendments deserve maximum scrutiny, not minimum: A "Fifth" amending act to a foundational pro-life law is not a minor technical tweak. Each prior amendment has shaped who the Foundation serves; this one deserves the same level of public examination as the original Act.

The unborn have no vote and no voice in this process: Legislative amendments to protective structures for unborn life are, almost by definition, decided without direct representation from those the law exists to protect. That places a heavier duty of care on lawmakers, not a lighter one.

Government-sponsored bills still require full accountability: This bill was brought by the Bundesregierung itself. That does not exempt it from rigorous, transparent review; if anything, a government-initiated change to a pro-life protection mechanism warrants closer public attention, not less.

Families cannot plan around uncertainty: Crisis pregnancy centres, mothers already receiving support and organisations that rely on the Foundation's stability need clarity about what is changing. Ambiguity itself is a harm when it concerns support that vulnerable families depend upon.

What is not yet debated can still be shaped: Because the bill has not yet been deliberated by the Bundesrat, there remains a window in which public pressure can insist on transparency and safeguards before the amendment is finalised.

### Why is it urgent that we take action now?

No fixed date has yet been set for the Bundesrat's deliberation, but that is precisely the danger, not the reassurance. Bills sent to the Bundesrat can be scheduled and moved through with little public notice once the process resumes, and by the time deliberation begins in earnest, the space for public input can already have closed.

Once this amendment is adopted, its changes to the Mother and Child Foundation become the settled legal framework. There is no simple mechanism to unwind a narrowed Foundation after the fact; whatever the Fifth Amendment locks in becomes the baseline every future government inherits.

This bill is the live test of Germany's decades-old promise that unborn children and the mothers carrying them would have somewhere real to turn instead of abortion. If that promise is worth anything, it must survive contact with a routine-looking amending act just as much as a headline-grabbing one.

If supporters of life stay silent while this moves through the Bundesrat, that silence will be read as public indifference to whatever changes are made. Lawmakers take note of who speaks up and who does not; an amendment that passes unchallenged is one the public is assumed to have accepted.

### Why would they listen to us?

The Bundesrat represents Germany's federal states, and state governments are directly answerable to voters who care deeply about family policy and support for mothers. A visible, organised wave of concern from citizens who value the Foundation's mission is difficult for state representatives to ignore, particularly on a bill that has not yet been debated and where public expectations can still shape the outcome. CitizenGO supporters have a track record of pushing pro-life concerns onto the public record precisely at this early, undecided stage, when a clear signal can still influence how thoroughly an amendment is scrutinised.

### Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:

If the Bundesrat allows this amendment through without demanding full transparency, the Mother and Child Foundation could see its support for pregnant women in crisis narrowed, its funding reduced, or its reach quietly limited, all without the public ever being told plainly what changed. Mothers who might otherwise have chosen life for their unborn child, because real help was there, could instead find that help diminished, and the child alongside her would bear the cost of that silence.

### Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:

If the Bundesrat insists on full public scrutiny before this amendment proceeds, and refuses to accept any changes that weaken the Foundation's support for mothers and unborn children, the Mother and Child Foundation continues to stand as a genuine, strengthened alternative to abortion. Women in crisis pregnancies keep, or gain, real practical support, and Germany's decades-old promise to protect both mother and child is renewed rather than quietly hollowed out.

### Ideas for eventual Offline Actions

Organise letter-writing after Mass or church services addressed to state representatives who sit in the Bundesrat, asking them to demand full transparency before deliberating this amendment. Coordinate with crisis pregnancy centres that rely on the Foundation to share first-hand accounts of what its support means, for use in briefings to state ministries. Hold a short prayer vigil outside a regional government building on the day the bill is expected to be tabled for deliberation, to keep public attention on the issue as it moves through the Bundesrat.
