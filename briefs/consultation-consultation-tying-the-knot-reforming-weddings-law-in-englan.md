**What are we asking for in the petition?**

We are asking supporters to respond to the Law Commission's consultation before it closes on 24 September, calling on Government to reject proposals that would let marriage be reduced to a celebrant-led or outdoor ceremony stripped of the legal safeguards that protect spouses, children and the institution of marriage itself.

**Why would they listen to us?**

This is a live, open Government consultation specifically inviting public responses, meaning ministers and the Law Commission are formally required to consider what supporters submit. Parliament has already shown sustained interest, with 9 debate contributions and 4 written questions on this issue in the past six months, proving MPs are watching this space closely.

**What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?**

Marriage is not just a ceremony, it is a legal bond that protects spouses and children through binding safeguards built up over generations. This consultation proposes loosening those safeguards in favour of informal, celebrant-led or outdoor ceremonies. If the legal backbone of marriage is hollowed out to make weddings more 'flexible', the people who lose out are the vulnerable: spouses left without proper legal protection and children whose family life rests on an institution now treated as optional paperwork.

## Prepare phase prompts

**What are some arguments supporting our point of view?**

- Marriage's legal safeguards exist precisely to protect people, especially women and children, when relationships break down. Weakening them in the name of flexibility punishes the vulnerable.
- A wedding is not merely a personal celebration; it is the public, legal foundation of family life. Detaching the ceremony from its legal force undermines the seriousness of the commitment being made.
- Reforms marketed as modernisation often quietly erode protections that took decades to build. Supporters should not assume 'reform' means improvement.
- Parliament has already seen sustained attention on this issue, with 9 debate contributions and 4 written questions in the last six months, showing this is a live and contested area of policy, not a settled technical matter.

**Why is it urgent that we take action now?**

The consultation closes on 24 September 2026 and responses must be submitted before then. Once it closes, the window for public and parliamentary voices to shape the outcome will be gone, and any resulting reform could be locked in for a generation. With recess approaching, this is exactly the moment officials hope fewer people will be watching.

**Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:**

Marriage law is reformed to permit celebrant-led and outdoor ceremonies with weakened legal safeguards, meaning couples may believe they are properly married when the underlying legal protections have been diluted. Spouses and children lose the certainty and protection that marriage law was designed to provide, and the institution of marriage is further reduced to a matter of personal preference rather than a protected legal and social foundation.

**Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:**

The Law Commission and Government hear a strong public response defending marriage's legal safeguards. Any reform that proceeds preserves, rather than dilutes, the legal protections for spouses and children, and marriage continues to be recognised and protected as more than a ceremony choice.
