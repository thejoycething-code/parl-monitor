**What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?**

Marriage is not just a ceremony, it is a legal bond that protects spouses, children and families. This consultation proposes opening the door to celebrant-led and outdoor ceremonies that risk detaching marriage from the legal safeguards that have protected couples and families for generations. Weakening those safeguards in the name of flexibility puts the very meaning and protection of marriage at risk.

## Prepare phase prompts

**What are some arguments supporting our point of view?**

- Marriage law exists to protect people, not merely to mark an occasion. Legal safeguards around who can marry, how, and under what oversight exist for good reason.
- Celebrant-led ceremonies without proper regulatory oversight risk creating confusion over what is and is not a legally binding marriage, leaving couples and families exposed.
- Outdoor and unregulated ceremony formats may sound appealing but they can weaken the state's ability to ensure marriages are entered into freely, lawfully and with proper record-keeping.
- Reform should strengthen protection for marriage as a public, legally accountable institution, not dissolve the distinctions that give it meaning and legal force.
- Family stability depends on clear legal foundations. Any reform that blurs the line between symbolic ceremony and legal marriage threatens that stability.

**Why is it urgent that we take action now?**

The consultation closes on 24 September 2026. Once it closes, the window for the public to have a say on how marriage law is reshaped will be gone. With debate already active in Parliament, nine debate contributions and four written questions on this issue in the last six months, the direction of reform is being actively shaped right now. Supporters must respond before the deadline or lose the chance to be heard.

**Why would they listen to us?**

This is a live, open Law Commission consultation. Government bodies conducting statutory consultations are required to consider public responses before recommending changes to the law. Sustained parliamentary interest, shown by ongoing debate contributions and written questions, means this issue is firmly on the radar of decision-makers, and a strong volume of public responses defending legal safeguards can genuinely influence the final recommendations.

**Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:**

If reform proceeds unchecked, marriage in England and Wales could be reshaped into a loosely regulated ceremony detached from proper legal safeguards. Celebrant-led and outdoor weddings without sufficient oversight could blur the line between a legally binding marriage and a symbolic occasion, weakening protections for spouses and children and eroding the institution of marriage itself.

**Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:**

If we succeed, the consultation response will reflect strong public demand to preserve marriage as a legally protected, properly safeguarded institution. Any reform to weddings law will maintain robust legal oversight, ensuring that marriage continues to carry the full weight of legal protection for couples and families, regardless of ceremony format.
