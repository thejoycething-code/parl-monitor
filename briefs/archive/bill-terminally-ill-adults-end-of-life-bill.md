**What are we asking for in the petition?**

We are calling on MPs to vote AGAINST the Terminally Ill Adults (End of Life) Bill at its next Commons stage, and asking supporters to write to their MP before the 2nd reading debate ahead of 11 September 2026 to demand they reject this dangerous legislation.

**Why would they listen to us?**

MPs are elected to represent their constituents, and on a matter of conscience like this, they are especially attentive to the depth and volume of public feeling. The sheer scale of parliamentary debate already logged shows this issue is live and contested, meaning MPs are actively weighing arguments on both sides. A clear, well-organised wave of constituent letters ahead of the 11 September 2026 date can and does shift undecided votes on conscience issues such as this.

**What is happening that we are responding to?**

Terminally Ill Adults (End of Life) Bill (sponsor: Lauren Edwards) is at 2nd reading in the Commons. Next key date: 2026-09-11. Parliamentary activity on this issue in the last six months (from the monitor's ledger): 345 debate contributions, 1 written questions.

**What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?**

This Bill asks Parliament to redefine the state's role from protecting life to facilitating death. The most vulnerable people in our society, the terminally ill, the disabled, the elderly, those in pain, those who feel they are a burden, are the very people this Bill puts at risk. Once the law says some lives are worth ending early, no safeguard on paper can truly protect those who feel pressured, however subtly, to ask for death rather than seek care.

## Prepare phase prompts

**What are some arguments supporting our point of view?**

Assisted dying laws elsewhere have repeatedly seen eligibility criteria widen over time, from terminal illness to disability to mental health, once the principle is conceded. Doctors are trained to heal, not to end life, and this Bill asks them to make life or death judgements under pressure and time constraints that risk error. Proper palliative care, not death on demand, is the compassionate answer to suffering. Family and disability rights groups have long warned that legalising assisted dying changes the relationship between doctor and patient, and between the state and the vulnerable, in ways that cannot be undone.

**Why is it urgent that we take action now?**

This Bill is now at 2nd reading in the House of Commons, with the next key date set for 11 September 2026. Parliament has already seen intense activity on this issue, with 345 debate contributions logged in just six months. That level of engagement shows this fight is far from settled, and every voice from concerned citizens matters now, before MPs cast their votes. Once a Bill of this kind clears its early Commons stages, momentum builds and it becomes far harder to stop.

**Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:**

If this Bill progresses, the United Kingdom will cross a line that cannot be uncrossed. Vulnerable, terminally ill people, including those who feel they are a burden on family or the NHS, may come to feel that ending their life early is expected of them. Doctors will be drawn into ending lives rather than focusing wholly on healing and care. What begins as a narrowly framed law for a small group of cases has, in other countries, expanded steadily to cover far more people over time. **Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:**

MPs reject the Terminally Ill Adults (End of Life) Bill at its Commons stages, sending a clear message that the answer to suffering is better care, not state-sanctioned death. The dignity and protection of every terminally ill and vulnerable person in Britain is upheld in law. Resources and political energy are redirected instead towards strengthening palliative care so that no one need face the end of life without proper support and comfort.
