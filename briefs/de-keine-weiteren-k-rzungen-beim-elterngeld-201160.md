### What are we asking for in the petition?

The German Bundestag and the Federal Government must abandon the plan to cut Elterngeld from twelve months to nine, and must halt the accompanying reductions to Unterhaltsvorschuss and other family supports, because parental benefit was created as a promise: that a mother or father would have the time, undivided and unhurried, to bond with a newborn, to settle a child into childcare, and to hold a family together through its most fragile season. That promise is not a line item to be trimmed when a budget runs short. Ministers who claim to champion family stability owe parents the months they were promised, not a smaller, meaner version of them handed down by decree.

### What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?

Two months that families cannot spare are being taken away: MPs in the Bundestag have pointed out that the planned cut falls exactly on the window many parents need for Kitaeingewöhnung, the gradual settling of a child into childcare, meaning the shortfall lands at the worst possible moment.

Single parents are targeted alongside couples: the proposed reduction does not spare Alleinerziehende, who already carry the heaviest burden of balancing work and childcare alone, and who have the least room to absorb a sudden loss of income support.

Family policy is being cut with a "Rasenmäher," a lawnmower, not a scalpel: as one member described it, indiscriminate across-the-board cuts to household budgets are being applied without steering or priority, meaning family support is sacrificed alongside unrelated spending simply because it is convenient to cut.

A pattern of shrinking support is emerging, not an isolated adjustment: alongside Elterngeld, cuts are also being made to Unterhaltsvorschuss, the Kindersofortzuschlag and Wohngeld, showing that this is part of a wider erosion of the financial floor beneath families, not a one-off saving.

Parents are being told this is "consolidation," but it is lived as loss: as one member put it plainly, the change means less time together, less flexibility, and more stress for parents who are already stretched thin.

### What are some arguments supporting our point of view?

Time with a newborn is not a discretionary benefit: the early months of a child's life shape attachment and stability, and cutting the period of paid leave forces parents back to work before that foundation is secure.

Childcare settling-in cannot be rushed by government decree: Kitaeingewöhnung takes the time it takes, and MPs have specifically flagged that the missing two months fall exactly in that critical window.

Single parents have no partner to absorb the shortfall: Alleinerziehende face the same proposed cut with none of the flexibility that a two-parent household might have, deepening inequality precisely where support is most needed.

Across-the-board cuts abandon any pretence of family priority: when reductions are applied like a lawnmower rather than through considered choices, family policy loses its place as a stated national priority and becomes just another casualty of a tight budget.

This is not an isolated cut but part of a wider retreat: reductions to Unterhaltsvorschuss, Kindersofortzuschlag and Wohngeld alongside Elterngeld show a government pulling back support for families on multiple fronts at once.

Ministers themselves have acknowledged families need reassurance: even voices within government have said publicly that they hope for "good outcomes" from the coming parliamentary deliberations, an admission that the current proposal falls short of what families deserve.

Cutting Elterngeld undermines the very demographic and family stability aims that other parts of government claim to pursue: shrinking support for parents while claiming to strengthen families is a contradiction that ordinary people can see plainly, whatever the budget language used to dress it up.

### Why is it urgent that we take action now?

The petition against these cuts is open for co-signature only until 6 October 2026. After that date, the window for public pressure through this channel closes and the parliamentary process moves forward largely without it.

Once a budget cut like this is written into law, it does not stay temporary. Family benefit frameworks tend to lock in for years, and a government that succeeds in cutting Elterngeld from twelve months to nine will find it far easier to cut further next time than to restore what was lost.

This is a direct test of the promise that family policy exists to give parents security, not just savings on a spreadsheet. Ministers have spoken of wanting outcomes that "make people brave" to start and grow families; a cut to parental leave in the very window families need it most is the opposite of that promise kept.

If families and their supporters stay silent now, the Bundestag and Federal Government will treat that silence as consent to a policy that squeezes new parents precisely when they are most vulnerable. Every signature before 6 October tells them otherwise.

### Why would they listen to us?

This is not a fringe complaint. MPs from across the political spectrum in the Bundestag, from Die Linke to the Greens, have already raised sharp objections to these specific cuts on the record, and even government-aligned voices have publicly hoped for "better outcomes" in the coming deliberations. A visible surge of public co-signatures adds pressure precisely where it already exists, and it hands sympathetic MPs the mandate they need to push back before the parliamentary process finalises these reductions.

### Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:

If the Bundestag and Federal Government proceed unopposed, new parents across Germany will find Elterngeld cut from twelve months to nine, losing exactly the weeks many families need to settle a child into childcare. Single parents will be hit without the cushion of a second income, and further reductions to Unterhaltsvorschuss and related support will compound the loss, leaving families with less time together and more financial strain at the most fragile point in a child's early life.

### Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:

The Bundestag rejects the proposed cut, Elterngeld remains at twelve months, and Unterhaltsvorschuss and related family supports are preserved in full. Parents keep the months they were promised, families get the time they need to settle a newborn and a young child into care without being rushed by a government balance sheet, and the principle holds that family policy is not the first thing sacrificed when budgets tighten.

### Ideas for eventual Offline Actions

Organise families and local church groups to write personal letters to their Bundestag constituency MPs describing what the lost two months of Elterngeld would mean for their own household. Hold a small visible gathering of parents with prams outside a constituency office to hand over printed copies of the petition ahead of the 6 October deadline. Encourage supporters to share short video testimonies about the value of the Kitaeingewöhnung period, tagging relevant committee members from the Ausschuss für Bildung, Familie, Senioren, Frauen und Jugend.

A tender, everyday image of a parent gently settling a young child into a nursery or childcare setting, perhaps mid-goodbye at the door, faces soft and a little anxious, hands still linked. The lighting should feel warm but slightly wistful, hinting at time running out. A subtle visual device, such as a calendar page or clock in the background showing months being crossed out, could reinforce the stolen-time theme without feeling heavy-handed.
