### What are we asking for in the petition?

The Bundestag must reject this bill outright, because the promise that underpins German democracy since 1949 is that the state does not punish an opinion, however wrong, however offensive, however dangerous it seems to those in power. Members of the Bundestag owe it to every citizen who has ever held an unpopular view to vote this proposal down rather than create a new speech-crime that makes denying a historical or political fact a matter for prosecutors and prison rather than for argument and rebuttal. No opinion is a crime, and the moment the state decides otherwise for one question, it has claimed the right to decide it for any question that follows.

### What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?

A new thoughtcrime is being invented: this bill would criminalise a specific opinion, not an act, turning what someone believes or says into a matter for the courts rather than the public square.

The existing law is already too vague to trust with more power: as AfD's Fabian Jacobi warned, Volksverhetzung is an "überdehnter und schwammiger" rubber paragraph, already stretched far beyond what its wording can bear. Layering a new speech offence on top of it multiplies that vagueness rather than fixing it.

The precedent will not stay contained: once the Bundestag accepts that denying one historical or political claim is a criminal matter, there is no principled line left to stop it criminalising denial of other claims that a future government finds inconvenient, including religious or moral convictions held by ordinary believers.

Dissent becomes a legal risk rather than a democratic right: citizens, journalists, academics and clergy who hold minority or conservative views on history, politics or faith would face the same tool that today claims to target only one form of denial.

The cure for bad speech becomes worse than the disease: as Ronald Gläser put it, "die Antwort auf Propaganda kann niemals Gegenpropaganda sein. Es gibt nur eine einzige Waffe gegen Desinformation: Meinungsfreiheit." Criminal law is a blunter and more dangerous instrument than open argument, and once used this way it does not stay narrow.

Trust in the state's neutrality is broken: a government that prosecutes opinion, rather than incitement to violence or genuine hatred, tells every citizen that their conscience is provisional, held only at the pleasure of whichever majority currently controls the Bundestag.

### What are some arguments supporting our point of view?

Free speech is the foundation, not an obstacle: a democracy that punishes opinion rather than answering it has already conceded that its own arguments cannot win in the open.

Vague laws are dangerous laws: MPs across parties, including from the AfD and the Greens, have already flagged how loosely drawn Germany's existing speech offences are. Adding a further criminal category compounds a problem lawmakers themselves acknowledge exists.

The state's data and surveillance record adds to the risk: Helge Limburg warned of the danger that prosecutorial data could fall into the wrong hands, a caution that applies directly to any new offence that hands the state more power to investigate what citizens believe and say.

History warns against state-enforced orthodoxy: Germany's own twentieth century is the clearest possible lesson in what happens when the state decides which historical and political opinions may be spoken aloud and which cannot.

Counter-speech, not criminal law, is the proven remedy: the strongest defence against denial, distortion or propaganda has always been open debate, evidence and rebuttal, not prosecution.

Cross-party MPs have already defended this principle: Andreas Audretsch of the Greens said defending freedom of opinion "ist in dieser Phase das, was wir Demokraten tun müssen", and CDU/CSU's Catarina dos Santos-Wintz argued the debating space must be protected "nicht gegen die Meinungsfreiheit, sondern gerade für unsere Meinungsfreiheit." This is not a fringe concern.

The slope runs directly toward faith and family: a state that can criminalise denial of one politically sensitive claim today can just as easily criminalise dissent from state orthodoxy on marriage, life or religious teaching tomorrow.

Selective enforcement is inevitable: laws that criminalise opinion are rarely applied evenly. They become tools used against whichever minority view the government of the day finds most inconvenient.

Once passed, a law like this is nearly impossible to repeal: criminal statutes acquire institutional inertia, prosecutorial habit and judicial precedent that make reversal far harder than prevention.

### Why is it urgent that we take action now?

This petition is open for co-signature only until 6 October 2026. After that date the window to add public pressure before the Bundestag moves further on this bill closes.

Once a speech-crime framework like this is adopted, it does not stay confined to its original target. It becomes precedent, cited by prosecutors and courts in every future case where the state wishes to criminalise an inconvenient opinion, and undoing that precedent afterwards is far harder than stopping it now.

This is a direct test of the promise every German lawmaker has made under Article 5 of the Grundgesetz, that opinion is free. MPs from across the political spectrum have already stood up in committee to say this principle must hold. Now they must prove it with a vote, not a speech.

If supporters stay silent now, the Bundestag will read that silence as consent. The time to be heard is before the vote, not after the law is already on the books.

### Why would they listen to us?

Members of the Bundestag have already spoken publicly on this exact bill, and the record shows real, cross-party unease about expanding speech offences, from AfD to the Greens to CDU/CSU. This is not a closed question inside the chamber, and public pressure at this stage can still shift undecided votes before the decision is made.

### Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:

The Bundestag passes this bill, and denying a specific claim about Israel's right to exist becomes a criminal offence in Germany. A precedent is set that the state may criminalise opinion, one that future governments can and will extend to other historical, political and moral questions, including views on marriage, life and faith that conservative and Christian citizens hold today.

### Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:

The Bundestag rejects the bill. No new speech-crime is created, the principle that opinion is not a crime holds firm, and Germany's lawmakers reaffirm that the answer to disagreement is argument, not prosecution.
