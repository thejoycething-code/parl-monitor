### What are we asking for in the petition?

We call on the Bundestag to reject this motion and to refuse any framework that hands children and teenagers "self-determined" access to hormones or surgery for gender incongruence without rigorous psychological assessment, sustained therapeutic support and the full involvement of their parents. Germany's lawmakers owe every child the protection of a childhood allowed to unfold before irreversible medical decisions are made in its place, and that duty cannot be discharged by relabelling medical caution as an obstacle to be legislated away.

### What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?

Children cannot consent to what they cannot yet understand: a ten or fourteen year old grasping for identity is being offered a route to permanent bodily change dressed up as self-determination, when true self-determination requires a maturity the law rightly withholds from minors in every other serious decision.

Parents are being written out of their own children's care: a motion built around "self-determination" quietly sidelines the parents who bear daily responsibility for a child's wellbeing, treating their guidance and consent as an inconvenience rather than a safeguard.

Irreversible harm is being reframed as a right: hormonal and surgical interventions can leave lifelong physical and psychological consequences, yet this motion pushes toward guaranteeing access rather than guaranteeing caution, inverting the precautionary principle that ought to govern paediatric medicine.

Desistance and doubt are being erased from the picture: many young people who experience gender distress in childhood or adolescence come to feel differently as they mature, and a system built to fast-track "provision" leaves no honest space for that possibility.

Clinical judgement is being replaced by political ideology: doctors and therapists who counsel caution risk being cast as obstructing a child's rights, when in truth they are the last line of defence against decisions no child should be pressured into making at speed.

### What are some arguments supporting our point of view?

Childhood is not the moment for irreversible choices: the law protects minors from voting, marrying, drinking and signing contracts precisely because their judgement is still forming, yet this motion would trust that same judgement with life-altering medical intervention.

The evidence base is far thinner than campaigners claim: several European health authorities, including in Sweden and Finland, have already pulled back from paediatric medical transition after reviewing the evidence and finding it wanting, a caution this motion ignores.

Detransitioners' experiences are being sidelined: growing numbers of young adults who transitioned as minors have spoken of regret and irreversible loss, testimony that a rights-framed motion has no room to hear.

Parental authority is a safeguard, not a barrier: German family law already recognises that parents hold responsibility for their children's welfare, and any framework that bypasses them in matters this serious undermines the family as the first protector of the child.

Self-determination is being misapplied to bodies that are still developing: genuine self-determination presumes a settled sense of self, something adolescence by its nature has not yet delivered, making this motion's premise philosophically as well as medically shaky.

Fast-tracked provision removes the space for watchful waiting: many clinicians internationally now favour patient, exploratory therapeutic support over immediate medical pathways, an approach this motion's language of "guaranteed provision" would squeeze out.

The state's duty of care outweighs activist pressure: lawmakers answer to every child, not only to the loudest campaigning voices, and that duty is best honoured by protecting children from decisions they cannot yet fully weigh.

### Why is it urgent that we take action now?

This motion has not yet been debated in the Bundestag, but the window for concerned voices to be heard narrows the moment scheduling begins, and silence now will be read later as consent.

Once a framework like this is adopted, it does not stay contained. Guarantees of "self-determination" and "provision" become the baseline against which every future safeguarding proposal is judged too restrictive, locking in a permissive system that becomes extraordinarily hard to unwind.

Germany has made commitments to protect children from harm and to uphold parental rights within the family, commitments this motion puts directly to the test. A vote that guarantees medicalised pathways for gender incongruence in minors, without robust safeguards, would mark a retreat from those very promises.

If those who care about children's wellbeing stay quiet while this motion moves forward, that silence will be counted as agreement. Members of the Bundestag need to hear, before any debate takes shape, that there is principled and organised opposition to a bill built on ideology rather than caution.

### Why would they listen to us?

Bundestag members outside the sponsoring Green faction remain persuadable, particularly those in coalition and opposition parties who have publicly worried about the medicalisation of childhood and about parental rights being eroded. A visible wave of concerned constituents, especially parents and clinicians, gives moderate voices grounds to push back before the motion advances, while it is still at the earliest possible stage.

### Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:

If the Bundestag allows this motion to advance unchallenged, children experiencing gender distress will be steered toward irreversible hormonal and surgical intervention with parents and cautious clinicians pushed to the margins, and the protective pause childhood is supposed to provide will be legislated away for a generation of German families.

### Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:

If the Bundestag rejects this motion, German law will continue to require that any support for children experiencing gender incongruence rests on thorough psychological care, honest space for a change of mind, and the involvement of parents who love and are responsible for their children, giving every child the right to grow up first before any irreversible step is taken.

### Ideas for eventual Offline Actions

Organise a letter-writing drive so constituents can write directly to their Bundestag representatives ahead of any debate, host a public information evening with a clinician or detransitioner willing to share evidence-based concerns, and coordinate with sympathetic German parenting and family organisations to deliver a joint open letter to committee members before the motion is scheduled for its first substantive reading.
