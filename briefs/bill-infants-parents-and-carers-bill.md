**Who is the petition addressed to?**

Sir Desmond Swayne MP, Sponsor of the Infants, Parents and Carers Bill

**What are we asking for in the petition?**

Sir Desmond Swayne MP must use his position as sponsor of the Infants, Parents and Carers Bill to guarantee, in black and white at Committee stage, that parents are recognised as the first and primary authority over their infant's care and upbringing. He owes this to every mother and father who has ever been told by a well-meaning professional that they know best: no carer, agency or state guideline should be permitted to override a parent's considered judgement about their own child without clear and compelling cause. This is the promise that a bill bearing the words "parents" in its very title should keep, and it is a promise worth fighting for while the Bill can still be amended.

**Why would they listen to us?**

Sir Desmond Swayne is the Bill's own sponsor, which means he has both the standing and the direct means to propose or accept amendments strengthening parental primacy at Committee stage. MPs sitting on the Committee are actively debating this Bill right now, as shown by the sheer scale of recent parliamentary activity around it, with well over a thousand votes and more than a hundred debates logged in the last six months alone. A Bill under this much active scrutiny is one where organised, clearly expressed public concern can still shape the outcome, because the clauses are not yet fixed.

**What is happening that we are responding to?**

Infants, Parents and Carers Bill (sponsor: Sir Desmond Swayne) is at Committee stage in the Commons. Next key date: 2026-10-14.

**What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?**

Parents risk becoming an afterthought in their own child's life: a bill that names "infants, parents and carers" together in one breath risks treating mothers and fathers as merely one voice among several, rather than the first and natural authority God and nature gave them.

The language of "carers" can quietly displace the language of "parents": when legislation blurs the line between those who love a child as their own and those who are simply employed or assigned to look after them, it opens the door to decisions being made about a child's welfare that sideline the very people most invested in that child's future.

Committee stage decisions are made with little public scrutiny: amendments are debated and voted on by a small group of MPs, often out of the public eye, and once the Bill leaves Committee its shape is largely fixed for the remaining stages.

There is no guaranteed backstop protecting parental primacy: without an explicit clause affirming that parents hold the first responsibility for their infant, the Bill leaves the door open for future guidance, regulation or interpretation to chip away at that responsibility piece by piece.

Families who already feel talked over by officials will feel it again: for parents who have experienced schools, health visitors or social services treating their wishes as an inconvenience, a Bill that fails to anchor parental authority in law simply confirms their fear that the state sees them as replaceable.

## Prepare phase prompts

**What are some arguments supporting our point of view?**

The family is the first and most natural school of a child's life: long before any carer, teacher or official meets an infant, it is the parents who have carried, fed and known that child, and law should reflect that reality rather than dilute it.

Britain has a long tradition of parental primacy in law: our legal and moral inheritance has always held that parents, not the state, bear the first duty of care for their children, and any bill touching infants must not quietly erode that inheritance.

Vague language invites future mission creep: terms that do not clearly distinguish "parent" from "carer" give officials and courts room to expand state involvement well beyond what Parliament may have intended.

Committee stage is precisely where such safeguards are won or lost: amendments proposed and voted on now will determine whether parental primacy is written into the Bill's bones or left to chance.

The volume of parliamentary attention this Bill has already attracted, with well over a thousand recorded votes and dozens of debates in the last six months, shows MPs are engaged and amendments are genuinely live.

Faith communities have long taught that parents answer first to God, not to government, for the raising of their children, and law should not obscure that accountability.

Silent legislation becomes settled practice: once a bill of this kind is on the statute book, its wording becomes the reference point for years of guidance and case law, however loosely it was drafted.

Parents who feel secure in their authority raise more confident, more secure children: legal clarity about who is responsible protects not just parents, but the wellbeing of the infants themselves.

**Why is it urgent that we take action now?**

The Infants, Parents and Carers Bill reaches its next key Committee stage moment on 14 October 2026. This is the point at which amendments are tabled, debated and decided, and it is happening now, not at some distant final vote.

Once a bill clears Committee stage, its core wording becomes very difficult to change. The clauses agreed in Committee travel largely intact through Report and Third Reading, which means this narrow window is the last real opportunity to insist that parental primacy is written into the Bill in plain terms.

This is the test of whether Parliament still believes that parents come first. A bill that puts "parents" in its title but fails to anchor their authority in its clauses would be a promise broken in the small print, however good its intentions sound on the surface.

If supporters stay silent now, MPs on the Committee will reasonably assume the public has no concerns about how this Bill defines the roles of parents and carers. Silence at this stage will be read as consent to whatever wording emerges, and there will be no second chance to correct it once the Bill has moved on.

**Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:**

If Sir Desmond Swayne and the Committee let this moment pass, the Infants, Parents and Carers Bill could leave Committee stage with parental authority reduced to just one consideration among several, on equal footing with "carers" and other actors. Mothers and fathers across the country would find that the law meant to protect their family instead diluted their standing, leaving future disputes over an infant's welfare more likely to be settled by officials than by the parents who love that child most.

**Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:**

If Sir Desmond Swayne acts now, the Infants, Parents and Carers Bill could leave Committee stage with parental primacy written into it in unmistakable terms: a clear, binding recognition that mothers and fathers hold the first responsibility for their infant's care and upbringing, protected against being quietly overridden by carers or officials. Families would have a Bill that finally lives up to its name, putting parents first, not last.
