### What are we asking for in the petition?

We call on the European Commission to withdraw its support for a Union-wide ban on so-called "conversion measures" and to make clear, in law and in practice, that no directive built on this Citizens' Initiative will be used to criminalise parents who guide their children, pastors who counsel their congregations, or believers who freely seek help to live according to their faith and conscience. The Commission owes every family and every church in Europe a promise that a well-meaning campaign against genuine abuse will not become a legal weapon that silences prayer, therapy and biblical teaching on sexuality and gender. Any measure that follows this Communication must be drawn so narrowly, and defined so precisely, that it cannot be turned against parental authority, pastoral care, or the right of a struggling person to seek the counsel and support they themselves have asked for.

### What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?

A door closed on those who want help: Someone who feels genuine conflict about their sexuality or gender identity and asks for guidance, whether from a pastor, a counsellor or a parent, could find that door bolted shut by law, with no path back to the healing they are actually seeking.

Parents recast as criminals: Mothers and fathers who simply raise their children according to their Christian convictions on sexuality and family could find their guidance reclassified as a banned "conversion measure", stripping them of the authority they are owed as parents.

Pastors silenced in the pulpit and the counselling room: Ministers who teach what scripture says about sexuality, or who pray with a struggling congregant, risk being treated as practitioners of an outlawed "measure" rather than as shepherds exercising their calling.

Consent erased for the sake of ideology: An adult who freely and repeatedly asks for support to align their life with their faith could be denied that support altogether, because the law would assume they cannot really consent to what they want.

A vague definition weaponised across borders: "Conversion measures" is left undefined and elastic enough to capture prayer, pastoral conversation, and therapy alike, meaning a ban drafted for one country's worst abuses could be exported across the whole Union to silence ordinary religious practice everywhere.

One worldview enthroned as law: This Citizens' Initiative treats a single view of sexuality and gender as beyond question, and asks the Commission to enforce it across twenty-seven states, leaving no room for families, churches or individuals who see things differently.

### What are some arguments supporting our point of view?

Genuine abuse already has remedies: Coercive or violent so-called "conversion therapy" is already unlawful under existing criminal and medical law in every member state; a new EU-wide ban is not needed to punish real abuse, only to sweep up lawful pastoral and parental conduct alongside it.

Vague language invites abuse of the law itself: Without a precise, narrow legal definition, "conversion measures" can be stretched by activist courts and regulators to cover ordinary prayer, sermons, private counselling and even a parent's bedtime conversation with their child.

Parental rights are a settled principle, not a footnote: The right of parents to direct the moral and religious upbringing of their children is recognised in international human rights law and cannot be quietly overridden by an EU Communication responding to a Citizens' Initiative.

Freedom of religion includes freedom to teach unpopular truths: Article 10 of the EU Charter protects the right to manifest religion through teaching and practice; a ban that criminalises pastoral guidance on sexuality strikes directly at that freedom.

Adults have the right to seek help they themselves choose: Denying support to someone who freely asks for it, on the assumption their consent cannot be trusted, is a paternalism that undermines personal autonomy rather than protecting it.

Precedent spreads fast once adopted: Once the Commission legitimises this framework through a formal Communication, national legislatures across the Union, including Germany's, will feel pressure to enact matching bans, entrenching the same vague definitions everywhere.

Faith communities are the real target, whatever the drafting says: Campaigners behind this Initiative have been explicit that they consider any suggestion that sexual behaviour or gender identity can be discussed, questioned or changed to be inherently harmful, meaning churches teaching biblical sexual ethics are squarely in the firing line.

The Commission has a duty to protect pluralism, not erase it: The EU's founding commitment to diversity of belief and expression is betrayed if it enforces, through this Initiative's logic, the view that one understanding of sexuality is the only lawful one to hold or teach.

Silence now becomes law later: A Communication that welcomes or advances this Initiative without firm safeguards for religious and parental freedom will be read by legislators as the Commission's green light, however carefully it is worded.

### Why is it urgent that we take action now?

The Commission's Communication on this Citizens' Initiative is the moment that decides whether Europe's future law on "conversion measures" protects real victims of coercion or instead outlaws prayer, pastoral care and parental guidance. Once the Commission signals support, even in careful diplomatic language, the framework begins to lock in: national parliaments treat it as cover to draft their own bans, courts treat it as an interpretive guide, and campaigners treat it as proof that their broadest definition of "conversion" has official Brussels backing.

There is no later stage at which the definitions get narrower. Communications like this one set the terms of the debate that follows; they are the foundation on which directives and national laws are built, and foundations are far harder to dig up once the walls are standing.

The EU has repeatedly promised that it respects the competence of member states over family law, education and religious practice, and that Charter rights to religious freedom and parental authority are not decorative. This Communication is the test of that promise: either the Commission holds the line on precise, narrow definitions that target only coercive abuse, or it lets that promise quietly dissolve into a one-size-fits-all ban.

If concerned citizens, parents and churches say nothing now, Brussels will read that silence as consent, and by the time a directive reaches national implementation, the space to object will have shrunk to almost nothing.

### Why would they listen to us?

The Commission has publicly committed to respecting subsidiarity in family, education and religious matters, and to upholding the Charter's guarantees of religious freedom and parental rights; a large, visible wave of concerned citizens across member states is exactly the pressure that has previously made the Commission narrow vague proposals before they become binding law. Members of the European Parliament who sit on the relevant committees also respond to volume when a Citizens' Initiative moves toward legislative follow-up, because they know a Communication that ignores millions of religious families risks becoming politically toxic before it ever reaches a vote.

### Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:

If the European Commission proceeds without firm safeguards, a parent in Munich or Manchester could be reported for guiding their own child, a pastor could face investigation for praying with someone who asked for that prayer, and a person genuinely seeking to live according to their faith could be told the law forbids them from getting the help they want. What began as a Citizens' Initiative against real abuse would end as a continent-wide instrument silencing Christian teaching on sexuality and family.

### Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:

The European Commission draws a hard line: any future measure targets only coercive, violent or fraudulent abuse, explicitly protects parental guidance, religious teaching and freely chosen pastoral support, and states plainly that consenting adults and the families who love them remain free to seek the help and truth they choose. The door to change stays open, for everyone who wants to walk through it.

### Ideas for eventual Offline Actions

Encourage supporters to write to their national MEPs urging them to press the Commission for narrow, precise definitions before any directive is drafted. Organise a joint letter from churches and pastoral counsellors across member states describing lawful pastoral practice that could be criminalised. Coordinate with allied European pro-family and religious liberty groups to submit a joint response during any public consultation period tied to this Communication. Hold prayer vigils outside national parliaments or EU representation offices highlighting real testimonies of people helped by voluntary pastoral support.
