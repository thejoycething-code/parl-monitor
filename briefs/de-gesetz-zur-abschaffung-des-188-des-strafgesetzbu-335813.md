### What are we asking for in the petition?

The Ausschuss für Recht und Verbraucherschutz, the Bundestag's Committee for Legal Affairs and Consumer Protection, now holds this bill after its referral and must complete its scrutiny and recommend the repeal of Section 188 StGB, because the special legal shield it gives to politicians against "insult" was never theirs to claim over their own critics. Ending it does not strip anyone of the ordinary protection every citizen already enjoys under German law. It simply removes the extra privilege that lets the same words draw a harsher penalty when aimed at a minister than when aimed at a neighbour, restoring the basic democratic promise that the powerful answer to the people, not the other way round.

### What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?

One law for the powerful, another for everyone else: Section 188 StGB carries harsher penalties for insulting a "person in political life" than for insulting an ordinary citizen. It creates two classes of protected speech, where criticism of the governed is treated lightly and criticism of the governors is treated as a serious crime.

Moral and religious critics bear the heaviest cost: Christians who speak out against a minister's position on abortion, marriage or gender policy can find their words treated as a criminal insult rather than as legitimate political challenge. The vaguer the offence, the easier it becomes to punish conviction rather than genuine abuse.

Vague wording invites selective enforcement: offences framed around subjective "insult" leave prosecutors and courts wide discretion over what counts as criminal. [VERIFY] which cases have been brought under this provision, but broad discretion of this kind has historically fallen hardest on dissenters, not on the politically connected.

The political class shields itself from the scrutiny it demands of others: politicians who vote themselves greater legal protection than the people they serve invert the basic democratic bargain, that those with power face more scrutiny, not less.

Silence in committee lets the two-tier system stand indefinitely: unless the Ausschuss für Recht und Verbraucherschutz acts, ordinary citizens remain liable to harsher punishment for criticising a politician than for any other insult, with no end date in sight.

### What are some arguments supporting our point of view?

Equal speech law is a democratic first principle: no elected official should enjoy greater legal insulation from criticism than the voters who elected them.

Free speech is democracy's immune system: Andreas Audretsch told the Bundestag that "defending freedom of opinion is what we democrats must do in this moment," a principle that cuts squarely against a law giving politicians special protection from criticism.

Freedom of speech, not censorship, answers bad ideas: Ronald Glaser argued in the chamber that "the only weapon against disinformation is freedom of speech," not counter-propaganda or extra penalties for criticising power.

A vague, ill-defined offence chills legitimate dissent: broad insult offences leave citizens uncertain where legitimate criticism ends and criminal liability begins, and that uncertainty favours self-censorship over honest debate.

Cross-party voices already recognise the stakes: Catarina dos Santos-Wintz of the CDU/CSU told the chamber that protecting the "space for debate" must be done "for our freedom of opinion," not against it, showing this concern is not confined to one side of the House.

Data misuse risk compounds the danger: Helge Limburg has warned of prosecutorial data falling into the wrong hands, a risk that multiplies when a special insult offence generates more prosecutions, more files and more targets.

Faith communities have a direct stake: pastors, campaigners and ordinary believers who challenge a politician's stance on life or family issues should not face a harsher criminal standard than if they had made the same challenge to a neighbour.

Repeal restores balance rather than removing protection: abolishing Section 188 does not strip politicians of the ordinary insult law that protects every citizen, it simply ends their extra privilege above it.

### Why is it urgent that we take action now?

This bill has now been referred to the Ausschuss für Recht und Verbraucherschutz, which can complete its scrutiny and recommend a vote at any point. There is no fixed date announced, but once a committee finishes its work and sends a recommendation to the floor, the window for public pressure narrows sharply.

A special insult offence for politicians has stood on the books for generations. Once a committee report settles the matter one way or the other, a law like this does not get revisited again for years, whichever way it falls.

This is a rare test of Germany's democratic promise that the powerful answer to the people, not the other way round. Members across party lines have already invoked freedom of opinion in the chamber as a democratic duty. Whether that promise is honoured with a vote to repeal, or quietly buried in committee, will show which of them meant what they said.

If people of conscience stay silent now, the committee will read that silence as consent to leave a two-tier speech law in place indefinitely, one that punishes criticism of the powerful more harshly than criticism of anyone else.

### Why would they listen to us?

Members from AfD to the Greens have already spoken in the Bundestag in defence of free speech as a democratic principle, which means the committee is not being asked to invent a new value but to apply one several of its own members claim to hold. Advancing this repeal costs the state nothing and asks only that politicians give up a legal privilege that was never theirs to claim over their own critics, a request that is hard to refuse without contradicting words already spoken on the record.

### Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:

If the Ausschuss für Recht und Verbraucherschutz lets this bill stall or waters down the repeal, Germany keeps a criminal law that punishes a citizen more harshly for criticising a minister than for insulting a stranger. Christians, pro-life campaigners and family advocates who challenge political figures on moral grounds will remain exposed to a charge their secular critics of ordinary citizens would never face.

### Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:

If the Ausschuss für Recht und Verbraucherschutz reports the bill favourably and the Bundestag repeals Section 188, every German citizen, believer or not, answers to the same standard of speech law whether their words are aimed at a neighbour or a minister. The chilling extra risk facing moral and religious critics of political power disappears from the statute book for good.

### Ideas for eventual Offline Actions

Prayer vigils held quietly outside Bundestag committee sessions on the days the bill is discussed. Letters to committee members that quote their own recorded words on free speech back to them. Simple explainer cards distributed outside churches setting out how Section 188 creates unequal speech law. A short video testimony series from Christians or pro-life advocates who describe the chilling effect of facing a harsher standard for criticising politicians than for any other speech.
