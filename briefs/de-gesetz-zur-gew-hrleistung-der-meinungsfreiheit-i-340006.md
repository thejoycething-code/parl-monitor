### What are we asking for in the petition?

The Committee for Law and Consumer Protection must report this bill out with its core purpose intact: a law that genuinely widens the space in which German citizens can speak, question and dissent online, not one that merely wears the words "free speech" while leaving censorship powers untouched or strengthened. Germany owes its citizens the freedom it has promised them since 1949, and a bill that claims to guarantee that freedom is tested by whether it actually removes the mechanisms, platform obligations, vague "hate speech" categories and takedown pressures, that have been used to silence lawful opinion. The Committee must not let this bill become a battleground for party point-scoring while the substance, real legal protection for ordinary citizens who post an unpopular view, is quietly hollowed out in committee. Free speech must not be rationed by decree, whether that decree comes from a platform's terms of service, a ministry's guidance, or a parliamentary compromise that sounds bold in debate and does nothing in law.

### What is the key point of injustice that is at stake here?

A bill about freedom could become a bill about nothing: when the governing parties treat the very words "free speech" as suspect because of who proposed them, the substance of the protection risks being gutted in committee regardless of its merits.

Citizens are already being silenced under vague categories: the debate itself confirms that undefined terms like "Hassrede" are being used as though they were criminal offences, when Germany's own criminal code already draws careful, specific lines around incitement and insult.

The Digital Services Act looms over every word of this bill: a national law meant to guarantee speech could be rendered meaningless or even contradicted by a European framework that was never debated with free speech as its primary aim.

Ordinary citizens have no say once the committee closes its doors: the public hears party slogans in the chamber, but the decisive work, what stays in the bill and what is stripped out, happens away from scrutiny.

Free speech is treated as a tactic rather than a right: members on multiple sides accused each other of insincerity rather than engaging with whether citizens' actual freedom to speak would be protected, leaving the core question unanswered.

### What are some arguments supporting our point of view?

Free speech is a foundational right, not a partisan prize: whichever party authors a bill defending it, the measure must be judged on whether it protects citizens, not on who proposed it.

Germany's own criminal code already distinguishes clearly: offences like Volksverhetzung and Beleidigung are defined with precision, so vague administrative categories of "hate speech" add confusion rather than clarity or protection.

International observers have already flagged shrinking space for free expression in Germany: a report quoted in the chamber itself states plainly that the room for free speech "is shrinking and being undermined by several negative trends."

A law that merely restates existing obligations changes nothing: if the bill does not actually roll back takedown pressure, platform liability regimes or vague moderation mandates, it offers citizens words without protection.

The Digital Services Act cannot be ignored in this debate: any national guarantee of free speech must reckon honestly with the European rules that already shape what platforms remove and why.

Dismissing a bill because of its sponsor is itself a form of censorship: refusing to engage with the substance of a free speech proposal on partisan grounds undermines the very principle of open debate the bill claims to protect.

Citizens who post an unpopular opinion deserve clear, predictable law: uncertainty about what counts as punishable speech chills ordinary conversation far more than any single piece of legislation can fix after the fact.

The right to disagree, not just the right to assert an opinion unopposed, must be protected in law: speech protections exist precisely so that minority and unpopular views are not filtered out before they are heard.

### Why is it urgent that we take action now?

The bill has now been referred to the Committee for Law and Consumer Protection, the stage at which real decisions about its wording and strength are made. Once the committee finalises its report, the substance of this bill, strong or watered down, will be effectively locked in before it ever returns to the floor for further debate.

This is the moment where legislative frameworks harden. Amendments made quietly in committee rarely attract public attention the way floor speeches do, yet they determine whether a "free speech" law actually restrains censorship or simply repeats the status quo in friendlier language.

Germany's constitution promises its citizens the right to express themselves freely. This bill is a direct test of whether that promise still means something when the proposal comes from a party other members are eager to dismiss.

If supporters stay silent now, the committee will hear only the voices already arguing past each other in the chamber. Silence from citizens who actually want their free speech protected will be read as indifference, and the bill's substance will be shaped entirely by parties more interested in scoring points than in securing real protection.

### Why would they listen to us?

Members of the Committee for Law and Consumer Protection must justify their report to the full Bundestag and to the public, and a visible wave of citizens insisting that free speech be protected in substance, not just in name, raises the political cost of hollowing out the bill. MPs who claimed in debate to care about free expression cannot easily ignore constituents who hold them to that claim.

### Describe a bad outcome if we do not win this campaign:

The Committee for Law and Consumer Protection waters the bill down to an empty gesture, leaving vague "hate speech" enforcement, platform pressure and the Digital Services Act's reach untouched, while ordinary citizens who post an unpopular opinion online remain exposed to takedowns, shadow-bans and legal uncertainty with no real recourse.

### Describe a good outcome if we do win this campaign:

The Committee for Law and Consumer Protection strengthens the bill so that it genuinely narrows censorship powers, sets clear and specific speech protections drawn from Germany's existing criminal code rather than vague new categories, and gives citizens real, enforceable grounds to challenge unjust takedowns of lawful speech online.

### Ideas for eventual Offline Actions

Organise a letter-writing drive directed at committee members, timed to land before the committee's report is finalised. Host a public briefing or webinar with a German free speech lawyer explaining how the Digital Services Act interacts with this bill. Encourage supporters to share short video testimonials of lawful posts that were wrongly removed, to put a human face on the stakes for committee members and journalists covering the debate.
